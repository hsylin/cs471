Cheaper monitoring is correct, but does not establish useful serving speedup

Completed: 18 full-model sessions on the reserved eight H100s, nine methods on each of LongBench and NuminaMath. All 288 measured and 288 warmup requests completed without request failures and matched their individual frozen output targets. The final run used no diagnostic middleware, profiler or history shadow. Source e6f85026ca22f2390deb6e4e58a3d6b8b6bb8ee6; protocol SHA e64bfa207c45ef1a090fc857ccdc16b170d076262261f99a291883f2171a8118.

The optimization preserves B3/B4 policy semantics and reduces the isolated monitoring-cycle cost, but the observed full-model improvements are below 0.4% in mean response. Neither optimized gate beats EPLB OFF. The parallel resident dispatcher is effectively tied with OFF on NuminaMath and slower on LongBench. No method meets the prespecified 5% useful-gain target against OFF on both workloads; the parallel resident's LongBench mean regression exceeds the 2% guard.

One group per dataset supports descriptive ratios only, without confidence intervals or significance. These are the same previously examined prompts and arrival calendars, not fresh generalization data. Output token counts are equal, but generated text and expert routes can differ; this is not identical-work or answer-quality proof. Startup and warmup are separate from serving timing.

What changed

B3-light and B4-light replace multiple per-step PyTorch conversion/masking/casting/scatter operations with one fused logical-count update, then directly reduce the circular demand history without copying the entire ring into chronological order. Both retain continuous collection, consecutive 1,000-engine-step windows, 5% predicted-gain threshold, 5% TV threshold for B4, native 3,000-step interval, native candidate generation, rank agreement, transfer backend and commit handling. Both dispatchers and original B1–B4 are unchanged controls.

Fifteen exact GPU snapshot cases passed against an independent NumPy oracle, including wraparound, dummy steps and changed maps. Both full-model pilots matched the original demand-history implementation at every observed proposal (steps 750 and 3750) on all eight ranks. An earlier rolling-sum prototype failed the oracle and was rejected; all failed attempts remain preserved. The admitted direct-reduction implementation's synthetic median charged cycle fell from 85.46 to 28.77 ms. That component result did not translate into a useful full-model gain.

What the results do and do not explain

  1. Logical-history maintenance is not the dominant end-to-end penalty in these tests. Its cheaper implementation barely changes response time. This narrows the explanation; it does not uniquely identify the remaining cost. Native recording/control, replica layout, dispatch work, transfers, scheduling and collective waiting are not all independently ablated here. Both B4 variants transfer zero expert payload but remain slower than OFF, so migration payload alone cannot explain their penalty.
  2. The latency diagnostic does not support a frontend fix. The earlier large LongBench resident slowdown did not recur, and rendering/submission costs were similar. Extra first-token delay appears after submission. The diagnostic cannot distinguish engine admission, scheduling, collective waiting and computation inside that interval. The parallel mapper reduces a small kernel cost; its NuminaMath mean improvement is only 0.23% versus OFF and completed elapsed is essentially unchanged.
  3. B4 beats B3 here mainly while rejecting every migration. Original B4 improves NuminaMath mean response by 4.78% versus B3; light B4 by 5.07% versus light B3, but the latter's elapsed advantage is only 0.08%. This is a total policy effect, not proof that TV selects beneficial migrations. A no-migration or cooldown control with matched monitoring is still needed to isolate the statistic's value.

Phase coverage and migration opportunities

Native summed request-phase durations classify LongBench as only modestly prefill-dominant: 53.44–53.69% prefill across arms. It is not an isolated prefill benchmark or proof of a compute/bandwidth bottleneck. NuminaMath is strongly decode-dominant: over 99.51% decode. LongBench's deliberately short outputs are not full-answer quality evidence.

No LongBench gate arm reached a proposal, including warmup. NuminaMath reached step 750 during warmup with incomplete history and two measured proposals at 3750 and 6750. Original B3 allowed 29 then 47 layers; light B3 allowed 29 then 48. Both B4 variants allowed zero. Exact algorithm semantics do not imply the same final trajectories: asynchronous timing, generated token identities and observed demand differ between independent runs.

Original B3 recorded 47,610,593,280 remote received expert-tensor bytes across ranks; light B3 recorded 46,336,573,440. Both B4 variants recorded zero. These counters cover observed commit enqueues and are not a complete network-byte or bandwidth measure. Lower bytes do not imply lower completed elapsed time.

At the final B3 cycle, all-rank completion was observed for 9 of 48 layers (original) and 8 of 48 (light); the other 39/40 layers had no all-rank commit enqueue observation. They remain unfinished/censored, although all already-enqueued events completed. All three B4 no-op cycles completed on all ranks. Do not confuse successful response completion or asynchronous draining with all proposed weights being committed. CUDA-event completion is not a full-weight hash; ordinary native B1/B2 logs do not prove commit completion.

Latency diagnostic

Separate instrumented sessions compare OFF, resident_v2 and original B3 on both datasets. An opt-in ASGI middleware records request receipt, render boundaries, engine submission, selected DP engine, first engine output and stream delivery, plus API event-loop lag. Records contain timestamps and opaque identifiers, never prompt text, output text or token arrays. These sessions are diagnostics, not final uninstrumented speed measurements. First engine output is not necessarily first text content. API spans share one monotonic clock; cross-process/client comparisons require verified host alignment.

The failed initial attempt exposed custom plugin metadata to OFF during diagnostic startup. It served no requests. The next deployment exposes only the repository root for the native middleware; custom bootstrap metadata remains isolated to custom arms. Failed artifacts are preserved.

Conclusion

The implemented monitor is a correct component optimization. This experiment does not establish a practically meaningful end-to-end speedup over EPLB OFF, or justify another dispatcher rewrite. Preserve the negative result. A next causal study should first isolate native recording/control and fixed replica-layout cost, and compare B4 to a matched-monitoring never-migrate policy before crediting the stability statistic. Those are proposed follow-ups, not measurements in this run.
