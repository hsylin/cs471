Full-model resident fix and proposal B1–B4: completed results

No practically meaningful speedup over EPLB OFF was demonstrated. The equivalent parallel planner reduced mapper cost, but its NuminaMath mean-response gain over OFF was only 0.30%, and LongBench mean response regressed 19.17%. B4 improved NuminaMath mean response 5.20% versus B3, but migrated no weights; this does not establish that the stability statistic selects better migrations than simply migrating less.

What the fix changed, and why it did not establish a win

The previous resident planner sorted up to 32 shared experts and processed them serially. The fixed placement connects four independent rank pairs. Resident v2 processes four pairs in parallel over eight iterations, preserving the same assignments and tie-breaking. It passed 110 GPU/oracle cases and all eight ranks’ pilot representative numerical and full-replica-weight checks. The synthetic eight-row mapper median fell 3.43→1.70µs; the real pilot rank 0 decode fused-mapper mean fell 2.83→1.86µs. These are kernel measurements, not serving-speedup evidence.

NuminaMath v2 versus v1: mean response−0.85%, p95−0.82%, elapsed−0.37%. Versus OFF: mean−0.30%, p95−0.23%, elapsed−0.04%. Those observations fall well short of the frozen 5% useful-gain margin. Against native B2, v2 was 12.97% lower in mean response and 3.28% lower in elapsed; beating EPLB ON did not imply a useful advantage over OFF.

LongBench v2 versus OFF: mean+19.17%, TTFT+33.25%, p95−0.22%, elapsed−0.03%. It also regressed 14.83% in mean response versus v1. Five requests (indices 1–5) each incurred approximately 705ms additional response delay versus OFF, almost entirely before first content; their send lag remained small. The mean-response difference was 216.86ms, of which native RUNNING accounted for 34.52ms. The remaining 182.34ms lies outside the recorded native RUNNING/WAITING intervals. This is a timing-boundary decomposition, not proof of a frontend, CPU, network, or scheduling cause. The logged JIT warnings we checked occurred during warmup; they do not establish JIT as the measured regression’s cause. We retain the entire episode and do not rerun or remove those requests.

The original pilot’s recorded GPU-duration budget was dominated by NCCL collectives (rank 0 about 71% for the long-input trace and 50% for the decode trace); the mapper was only 0.29% and 1.42%. Sums may overlap or include waiting and are not a critical-path or bandwidth-saturation proof. Thus the small avoidable mapper cost was a defensible bounded optimization, but the main cause of the LongBench response penalty remains unresolved. For identical logical routing, both OFF and resident use one active copy per expert; resident’s opportunity over OFF is redistribution between ranks, not reducing the global number of active experts.

Proposal B1–B4

B1 enables native EPLB without extra replicas. B2 adds 32 replicas. B3 adds a per-layer predicted peak-load improvement threshold of 5%. B4 additionally requires total variation≤5% between consecutive 1,000-engine-step logical-demand windows. Both gates pay identical continuous collection overhead; native async cadence remains 3,000 steps. Rejected layers return their existing map and retain native no-op bookkeeping. The same rule is applied independently to 48 layers.

LongBench had zero migration-decision opportunities for both gates, including warmup. Its B4/B3 mean difference−0.57% cannot demonstrate stability selection. B2 versus B1 increased mean response 1.01%.

NuminaMath B2 versus B1 increased mean response 2.40% and elapsed 0.22%. B4 versus B3 reduced mean response 5.20%, p95 5.16%, and elapsed 0.87%. B4 versus native B2 reduced mean 6.49% but increased elapsed 0.53%. B4 was still 7.12% slower in mean response than OFF. The B4/B3 mean crosses the 5% descriptive margin in this one episode, but neither reproducibility nor a generally useful policy is established.

Both gates had one incomplete-history proposal during warmup and two proposals during measured requests (engine steps 3,750 and 6,750). B3 admitted 47 and 23 layers; B4 admitted none (76 unstable and 20 insufficient-gain layer decisions across the measured proposals). Recorded B3 received expert-tensor bytes totaled 69,438,799,872 across ranks; B4 recorded zero. This supports an observed full-policy effect with fewer migrations. It does not isolate the information value of TV from a simpler low-frequency/no-migration policy, and predicted token balance is not measured runtime payback.

Commit limitation: B3’s first full-history cycle completed all 48 layer commits on all ranks. Its last cycle recorded 15 all-rank layer enqueues and only 14 observed all-rank GPU completions;33 layers had no enqueue record, and one further layer’s completion remained unobserved. Across all cycles,880 of 888 rank/layer enqueues had observed GPU completion; eight remain censored. B4 recorded 1,152 completed rank/layer no-op commits. These events are distinct from native async drain and do not hash final model weights. Native B1/B2 ordinary logs do not prove successful final commits. Serving completed, but full final committed-state and long-horizon payback claims remain unsupported.

Acceptance and limits

The proposed replacement fails the frozen useful-gain/regression criteria: no 5% mean gain over OFF and a LongBench regression exceeding the 2% margin. Preserve v1 as the prior control; v2 is an experimental optimization, not a validated replacement. B4 is a preliminary NuminaMath policy result, not proof of selective migration quality. No capacity or full-answer-quality claim is made.

Inputs, arrivals, rates, order, output targets, thresholds and replica budget were frozen before final measurements. We reused existing content/calendars; this is not fresh-content generalization. Equal counts do not force equal tokens or routes: v2/OFF output hashes matched 11/16 LongBench and 1/16 NuminaMath requests. Greedy generation and representative numerical checks do not establish all-logit equivalence. Fourteen sessions are fourteen configurations, not fourteen independent replicates. There were no recorded KV-cache preemptions. Earlier negative and natural-EOS results are retained.

The next justified diagnostic would timestamp API receipt/tokenization, engine submission, first scheduling and first output on the same clock to localize the five-request pre-first-token delay. A separate identical-state allow/skip replay or cooldown comparison would test whether TV selects migrations with better realized payback. Neither was added after observing this frozen run.
