Cost isolation and packed activation dispatch

Completed: 16 full-model sessions on the same eight H100s. The decode-heavy workload shows a modest observed end-to-end gain; LongBench does not. The parallel resident dispatcher with packing lowered NuminaMath mean response by 3.69% versus the same dispatcher, and by 4.14% versus unmodified EPLB off. Packing also lowered EPLB-off mean response by 4.02%, so the improvement is primarily a general transport optimization. The resident-plus-packing advantage over OFF-plus-packing is only 0.13% in mean response and 0.03% in elapsed time.

These are descriptive results from one fixed group per dataset, with no confidence intervals or significance claim. No method achieved the predeclared 5% useful mean-latency gain against unmodified OFF. The resident-plus-packing LongBench mean remained 2.22% above OFF, exceeding the 2% regression margin against that baseline. B3 packing did exceed 5% improvement against its own slower B3 baseline, but remained 8.10% slower than OFF on NuminaMath.

The fix losslessly packs each token’s BF16 hidden state, FP32 routing weights and int32 expert IDs into 1,040 int32 words (4,160 bytes), performs one native all-gather, and unpacks into the original types. It changes neither precision nor payload bytes. Native vLLM already groups its three gathers; the candidate combines their payloads. It activates only while capturing a CUDA graph with exactly one padded row per rank. Eager execution, larger shapes and unsupported extras retain native dispatch; native reduce-scatter is unchanged. No gate thresholds, placement or resident-selection algorithm were tuned in the final run.

All arms used identical previously selected inputs, absolute arrival calendars and per-request output targets from the earlier natural-EOS OFF run. LongBench produced exactly 162 tokens and NuminaMath exactly 17,519 tokens per arm, over 16 requests each. Normal EOS was ignored and each request’s minimum and maximum output length were fixed to its target. These are reused inputs, not fresh generalization evidence. All 256 measured requests and 256 warmup requests completed; no failed requests were dropped.

What caused the slowdown?

Verified: the largest profiled GPU-time category was activation communication. The matched fixed-map pilots show modest decode penalties: recording plus native window maintenance added 0.44% with zero replicas and 0.87% with 32 replicas; changing the fixed native configuration from zero to 32 replicas added 1.71%. These numbers are pilot contrasts, not additive percentages explaining the full final penalty.

Limits of isolation: recording contrasts include router atomics and copy/reset of the native history window. Zero versus 32 replicas changes ownership, capacity and expert work, not just memory allocation. Balanced versus native placement also changes which experts are duplicated. Resident versus native dispatch additionally removes native per-step bookkeeping. Equal output lengths do not fix expert routes. LongBench pilot timings varied widely and did not show a consistent recording penalty. The B4 attribution pilot used an expensive history shadow and is excluded from fair cost timing comparisons.

Deduction supported by the final comparison: small-message collective overhead is a useful optimization target for decode. The same packing change helps OFF, B4 and the resident method by roughly 4% in mean response. It does not explain away the remaining gate penalties: packed B4 remains 2.18% slower than OFF and packed B3 8.10% slower. We have not established a unique cause for every residual millisecond, nor a unique advantage for the resident policy over an equally optimized OFF baseline.

NCCL kernel duration includes peer waiting and synchronization; it is not wire time. The separate eight-GPU replay removed expert compute and validated arbitrary-bit payloads. With sixteen collectives per CUDA graph, small-shape dispatch was 43.31 μs native versus 35.01 μs packed, including packing/unpacking. Eager small-shape packing regressed (44.24 → 60.34 μs); the largest-shape graph also regressed (7,198 → 7,864 μs). The frozen fallback preserves native execution on those paths. Three technical repeats are not independent-run uncertainty.

Migration decisions and completion

LongBench had zero proposal opportunities, including warmup, so it cannot validate stability information. NuminaMath had an incomplete-window warmup cycle at step 750 and two measured cycles at 3,750 and 6,750. B4 and packed B4 rejected all 96 measured layer proposals (95 unstable, one insufficient gain), moved no expert tensors and completed all observed no-op commits. This is consistent with avoiding migration cost on this workload, but does not prove that TV selects better migrations than a cooldown or improvement-only policy.

B3 admitted 70 measured layer proposals; packed B3 admitted 77. Recorded received expert payload was 57.77 GB and 53.86 GB respectively across ranks. At the final cycle, only 2/48 B3 layers and 14/48 packed-B3 layers had a commit observed on every rank; 46 and 34 layer proposals respectively were not enqueued on every rank before closure. Every recorded enqueue had an observed CUDA-event completion, but the remaining proposals are censored. Drain is not commit proof. Consequently B3’s 6.19% mean-response improvement is an overall policy-run effect, not a clean estimate of packing time alone.

Correctness, failures and independent checks

All four candidate model pilots passed all-eight-rank bitwise dispatch shadows. Gate pilots matched the original consecutive logical-demand history; the resident pilot passed representative native-expert numerical checks and full replica-weight hashes. All 64 candidate traces matched remote bytes, and packing/unpacking actually executed in every rank/arm decode trace. The earlier eight attribution pilots and 128 traces are preserved. Full all-logit equivalence and answer quality were not tested.

The initial standalone communication replay and its diagnostic retry failed NCCL P2P initialization with CUDA error 101 because each Slurm process saw only one GPU. Both failed attempts remain archived. Matching the serving workers’ four-visible-GPU namespace fixed startup; native and paired candidate replays then passed. No successful model run was restarted or selected by speed.

Final closure verified 662 raw files against stable remote inventories, 94 controller source files and 99 protocol source files against Git. All sixteen runtime identities matched. Every request’s target length, completion, timing arithmetic and native phase count was checked. Fresh local CPU reconstruction and cluster-side CPU reconstruction match the report exactly after run-directory string normalization. The completion receipt and supplementary audit retain the evidence and censored commits.

Reproduction and records

From the repository root, with Python 3.10+ and NumPy (audit environment: 2.5.4), reconstruct all published serving metrics without GPUs or private prompts:

python -m full_serving.packed_analysis final \
  results/full-serving/cost-isolation-v1/packed-run-v1 \
  --output /tmp/cs471-packed-reconstructed.json

GPU repetition requires the pinned checkpoint, eight-H100 topology, exact environment and reconstructed private inputs identified by the existing dataset manifests. Use the frozen protocol and full_serving.packed_experiment; do not reselect prompts, calendars, lengths, thresholds or the small-graph rule. Full serving results use one group; a new independent group is required to assess reproducibility. Gzipped profiler payloads are retained locally/cluster-side and excluded from Git; their hashes and derived summaries are published. Public raw files contain IDs, hashes, counts and timings, not prompt/output text or token arrays.
