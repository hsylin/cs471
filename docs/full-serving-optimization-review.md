The most promising next step is to remove work that cannot repay its cost, while first locating the unexplained delay before engine execution. The existing results do not justify another elaborate dispatcher. They support a cheaper control path for B3/B4, a migration rule based on realized payback, and a resident dispatcher that can cheaply retain a static assignment when adaptation has no useful headroom. These are proposed changes, not new measured speedups. The review does not modify the frozen policies or launch another GPU experiment.

**What the completed experiment establishes**

The audited experiment used Qwen3-30B-A3B, eight H100 94 GB GPUs on two nodes, DP8/TP1/EP8, BF16, and the pinned vLLM AgRs allgather/reducescatter backend. This is full-model execution, not the earlier replicated-input single-layer harness. Each dataset had one group of sixteen requests per arm. All 224 measured requests completed with exact per-request output targets: 162 output tokens per LongBench arm and 17,519 per NuminaMath arm. Inputs and arrival schedules were matched; generated token identities and subsequent routing were not identical. These are descriptive observations from one group, not statistically established gains.

| Method | LongBench mean response | NuminaMath mean response | NuminaMath change versus OFF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Native OFF | 1.131 s | 10.635 s | Reference |
| B1: native EPLB, no extra replicas | 1.137 s | 11.898 s | +11.87% |
| B2: native EPLB, 32 extra replicas | 1.149 s | 12.183 s | +14.56% |
| B3: B2 plus improvement gate | 1.152 s | 12.017 s | +12.99% |
| B4: B3 plus stability gate | 1.145 s | 11.393 s | +7.12% |
| Original resident dispatcher | 1.174 s | 10.693 s | +0.55% |
| Parallel resident dispatcher | 1.348 s | 10.603 s | −0.30% |

The parallel dispatcher reduced NuminaMath mean response by 0.85% versus the original, but improved completed elapsed time by only 0.04% versus OFF. Its LongBench mean response regressed 19.17% versus OFF. B4 improved NuminaMath mean response 5.19% versus B3, but remained 7.12% slower than OFF. B4 also had slightly longer completed elapsed time than B2 despite its lower mean response. Different requests determine a mean, a tail, and the last completion; these metrics need not rank methods identically. [Audited report](full-serving-proposal-results.html), [machine-readable measurements](../outputs/full-serving/proposal-followup-comparison.json).

LongBench had only a modest prefill majority, approximately 53–55% of summed native prefill/decode request durations; NuminaMath was over 99% decode by that definition. These are phase-duration observations, not proof of hardware compute or bandwidth saturation. LongBench's deliberately short outputs do not establish answer quality. The serving elapsed boundary includes admitted-request drain, but excludes server startup, warmup and any outstanding control completion after the last response.

We have not identified one proven bottleneck shared by all arms. In LongBench, the parallel dispatcher's additional mean response time was about 217 ms versus OFF, but the additional native RUNNING duration was only about 35 ms. Roughly 182 ms remains outside recorded RUNNING/WAITING intervals. Five requests account for a conspicuous first-token delay; their client send lag is too small to explain it. This is an accounting residual, not a measurement identifying CPU scheduling, GIL contention, tokenization, networking or JIT as the cause. Warmup JIT warnings alone do not prove compilation during measurement. [Post-hoc diagnostics](../outputs/full-serving/proposal-followup-diagnostics.json).

The GPU planner itself is faster: the representative profiled fused-mapper mean fell from 2.827 to 1.861 microseconds, and 110 exact GPU/oracle cases passed. However, the original mapper represented only about 0.29% of LongBench and 1.42% of NuminaMath summed rank-0 kernel durations in the pilot. Collectives occupied much larger fractions. Those sums overlap and may include waiting, so they are neither an end-to-end Amdahl bound nor proof that network bandwidth is saturated. They do show why another planner-only optimization is a weak first bet.

**What the literature changes**

Balancing token counts is an incomplete runtime objective. An expert with a few tokens still incurs a weight-access and launch cost; larger groups incur rounded tile work. Consequently, spreading tokens across more copies can make execution slower even when rank token counts become more equal. METRO is the closest precedent for concentrating work on fewer active replicas: its real-system evaluation uses the same model family on eight A100s, with NuminaMath and InstructCoder, but uses a single NVLink domain and compares against EPLB rather than proving superiority to EPLB-disabled serving. Our one-home assignment principle is therefore incremental rather than novel. [METRO, §§IV–VI, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09277v1).

A cost model must also know when concentration becomes harmful. TEMPO describes activation floors, tile rounding and communication together, and reports failures of both token-only and activation-only objectives. Its implementation discussion is particularly relevant: counting and dispatch are fused, and solving is moved outside the serving process. Its reported GIL problem motivates an experiment here; it does not diagnose our residual. Its FP8 kernels, anonymized testbeds and model-dependent results cannot supply a numerical threshold for our BF16 H100 deployment. [TEMPO, §§2–5 and Appendix D, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13057v2).

This nonlinear-cost argument predates those preprints. MegaScale-Infer includes a floor for cold-expert cost in its balancing objective, but evaluates a substantially different disaggregated system and separates phases in important measurements. MegaBlocks demonstrates the importance of padded expert work in training; its sparse-metadata-excluding kernel benchmarks are not fully charged serving latency. Together they support measuring actual execution cost, without establishing that their larger architectural changes will help our small expert groups. [MegaScale-Infer, §§6–7, SIGCOMM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3718958.3750506), [MegaBlocks, §§4–6, MLSys 2023](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5a54f79333768effe7e8927bcccffe40-Abstract-mlsys2023.html).

Migration must earn back both its transfer cost and its interference with useful work. SmartMoE already filters small predicted improvements, prefers similar placements on ties and studies search/collection frequency. Its setting is training, so this is prior art for the control principle rather than validation of our TV statistic. FreeBalance instead budgets swaps using measured transfer cost and available attention overlap. Its evaluation is predominantly fixed-batch, long-input prefill on eight A800s; attention overlap should not be assumed available during our decode-heavy workload. The practical lesson is to bound migration and measure payback before adding a predictor. [SmartMoE, §6.2 and §7, ATC 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc23/presentation/zhai), [FreeBalance, §§3.3–4, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14205v1).

Better decisions can lose when the decision machinery blocks execution. Lina's inference ablations separate prediction, corrective scheduling and exposed overhead. COMET treats communication dependencies, host launch cost and competition for GPU resources as an integrated scheduling problem. Tutel avoids expensive parameter-layout changes when adapting execution, while DeepSpeed-MoE combines parallelism and specialized kernels. These results favor reducing unnecessary data movement and control work; their full-system headline gains should not be attributed to a replacement replica mapper. [Lina, §§5–7, ATC 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc23/presentation/li-jiamin), [COMET, §§3–5, MLSys 2025](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/e27ea0cd50b798ff8942caf9203f0992-Abstract-Conference.html), [Tutel, §§3–5, MLSys 2023](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5616d34cf8ff73942cfd5aa922842556-Abstract-mlsys2023.html), [DeepSpeed-MoE, §5, ICML 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/rajbhandari22a.html).

Response latency depends on the serving schedule as well as expert execution. Orca separates iteration scheduling from engine execution; PagedAttention demonstrates that a slower isolated kernel can coexist with better serving performance through memory and batching effects. Sarathi-Serve exposes the trade-off between prefill efficiency and decode stalls, while Llumnix separates migration downtime from overlapped work. In our run there were no KV preemptions, so a KV-capacity explanation lacks supporting evidence. First instrument the actual request boundaries rather than introducing a new scheduler based only on a literature analogy. [Orca, §§3–6, OSDI 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yu), [PagedAttention, §§6–7, SOSP 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613165), [Sarathi-Serve, §§4–5, OSDI 2024](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/agrawal), [Llumnix, §§4 and 6, OSDI 2024](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/sun-biao).

Recent work makes that coupling explicit. Gimbal jointly considers frontend pressure and source-aware expert placement, using four H100s and a different DP/TP layout. It reshapes BurstGPT request lengths; that is not the same as replaying authentic content with original timestamps. The manuscript bears an ATC 2026 header, but an independent publisher record was not established in this review, so its results are treated as manuscript evidence. ELDR, another preprint, routes requests among separate decode workers using expert-locality signatures. With our AgRs path, merely moving a request between DP ranks may leave the union of gathered expert work unchanged; importing ELDR would require a different deployment architecture. [Gimbal, §§4–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15177v1), [ELDR, §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00466).

A forward citation search also found MegaFlux, which jointly plans and pipelines replicas. Its planner sometimes avoids replication at small sizes, directly supporting the need for a no-action option. However, its full-model prefill experiment uses eight B200s in one NVLink domain and measures completion of one output token; its large-batch and training results do not establish decode-response gains on two H100 nodes. [MegaFlux, §§3–4 and appendices, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00671).

Open-source and engineering material provides implementation candidates, not independent validation. Meta's MetaShuffling emphasizes graph-compatible indexing and grouped execution, but its Llama-4 example is top-1, unlike our top-8 model. PyTorch's locality-aware MoE article includes precision and correctness qualifications, so its GEMM speedups are not a ready BF16 serving patch. DeepEP is a possible transport experiment, but changing AgRs also changes the globally gathered routing information used by our dispatcher. Compatibility with the exact pin and RDMA topology must be demonstrated, and the backend must be changed for all compared arms. [MetaShuffling](https://pytorch.org/blog/metashuffling-accelerating-llama-4-moe-inference/), [PyTorch MoE kernel study](https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerating-moe-model/), [DeepEP](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepEP), [vLLM backend guidance](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/serving/expert_parallel_deployment/).

**Three ranked optimization tracks**

**1. Make monitoring and rejected proposals cheap without changing decisions.** B3 and B4 currently force continuous collection, maintain a 94.48 MiB logical-demand ring per rank, and reorder/reduce the ring when proposing a change. Both use the same collection, which is a useful existing control. The code also performs host copies, a CPU gate, rank agreement, detailed JSON emission, and native asynchronous no-op bookkeeping when a layer is rejected. Zero transferred bytes therefore does not mean zero balancing overhead. The observed approximately 29–30 ms host-plan-and-gate duration per cycle excludes subsequent JSON emission and does not identify exposed serving delay. [Implementation](../full_serving/proposal_gate_plugin.py).

The lowest-risk candidate is a fused update of logical counts with preallocated buffers and exact rolling window sums. A circular history can still support the two consecutive windows, but avoid reconstructing a full ordered 2,000-step tensor at each proposal. This trades occasional reductions for per-step updates, so it must be benchmarked rather than presumed faster. Preserve dummy-step handling, padding, overflow safety, and conversion under the map that actually produced the counts. Naively resetting every 1,000 steps changes semantics: proposals begin at step 750 and may be delayed by asynchronous execution. Compare exact window counts, decisions and accepted maps against the existing implementation, including adversarial boundary cases.

Buffer diagnostic records and flush them outside critical execution while retaining the information needed for rank agreement and commit auditing. A genuine all-rank no-op fast path is a second, higher-risk change: it must retire the native cycle normally without discarding pending transfers or fabricating completion. Returning the old map is not that fast path. Test partial acceptance, fully rejected cycles, shutdown and delayed completion explicitly. Keep original B1/B2 untouched as native baselines; label any optimized versions separately.

**2. Make migration admission depend on runtime payback and bounded movement.** During measured NuminaMath execution, B3 accepted 47 then 23 layers; B4 accepted none. B3 recorded 69.44 GB of received expert-weight payload across ranks, approximately 64.67 GiB. This is payload accounting, not measured wire traffic. At its last cycle only 14 of 48 layers had observed all-rank GPU completion; 33 had no enqueue and one further layer remained unobserved after enqueue. Missing completion stays censored. B4's no-op events completed, but that does not establish useful migration selection.

A candidate rule should ask whether estimated future execution savings exceed exposed transfer, control and interference costs, with a safety margin. Preserve the current map as a zero-action candidate; prefer sparse changes that retain local slots, and cap bytes or moved experts per cycle. Do not merely raise the gain threshold or lower TV until these final traces look favorable. The horizon must be estimated from information available online or fixed on pilot data, not the known remaining benchmark duration. This is a new policy, separate from policy-preserving engineering.

TV is currently a weakly identified signal. Every complete B4 layer window had TV above 0.05: about 0.158–0.299 at step 3750 and 0.121–0.156 at step 6750. The first layer's total assignments changed from 42,544 to 10,720 at the former cycle, a roughly 75% decrease that normalized TV alone cannot express. Sampling variation, correlated top-8 choices, changing numbers of active requests, hotspot switches inside a window and delayed adaptation after a persistent change all matter. Two aggregate histograms cannot retrospectively identify these effects. B3 also predicts equal replica sharing, not measured runtime under the actual dispatch and padded kernels. [Reproducible record audit](../outputs/full-serving/optimization-review-audit.json).

The decisive test is a few identical-state forced-allow/forced-skip continuations at proposal boundaries, with a fixed future input/route sequence for mechanism isolation. Include transfer and completion costs and measure cumulative runtime until the move pays back or the horizon ends. Use one simple pilot-frozen cooldown as the alternative explanation. Teacher-forced or fixed-route replay is diagnostic only; the final comparison must still generate outputs end to end. If TV adds no decision information beyond predicted gain and cooldown, drop the claim about stability even if the conservative policy is faster.

**3. Give the resident dispatcher a cheap static option, then reassess placement.** Both implementations use the same fixed map and decision policy. Ninety-six experts have only one copy; the remaining 32 connect rank pairs (0,1), (2,3), (4,5), (6,7). Neither implementation can rebalance work between those four components. OFF already executes one physical copy per selected logical expert, so our dispatcher cannot reduce the global active-copy count below OFF for an identical routed batch. Its opportunity is to redistribute the slowest rank's work without paying more to make that decision than it saves. [Placement and oracle](../full_serving/resident_policy.py), [parallel implementation](../full_serving/resident_pair.py).

First measure identical-input execution under static one-home mapping, an offline precomputed assignment and the fully charged online assignment. The precomputed assignment is a headroom diagnostic, not a deployable baseline. If even that diagnostic cannot yield a useful gain, stop tuning the online planner. Otherwise use one cheap pilot-selected fallback, preferably based on already available shape or counters, and charge feature collection. A static one-home fallback retains the resident layout; it is not the same as OFF's 16 slots per rank, nor the same as native hash dispatch across replicas. Native hash can activate additional copies.

Only if assignment headroom exists should we test pilot-derived hot-expert placement within the same 32-replica budget, frozen across both final datasets. Replicating arbitrary expert IDs is not necessarily a good use of the budget, but a map chosen from final traces would invalidate generalization. Any placement change needs a matching static-map control. A small calibrated expert-cost model could later consider activation floors and tile work, but a complex solver should not precede evidence that online decision cost can be repaid. The original dispatcher remains the decision oracle; the parallel version is an implementation improvement, not a demonstrated end-to-end winner.

**Per-method consequences**

| Method | Most useful next change | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Separate native collection, solving and exposed migration costs; test sparse/cost-aware movement in a separately named variant | Fixed-state moves do not repay their cost within a useful horizon |
| B2 | Same control audit, plus measure whether extra active copies and padding outweigh load relief | Static replica dispatch is already slower than OFF, with no useful imbalance headroom |
| B3 | Exact cheaper monitoring first; bounded payback-based admission second | Monitoring contributes little, or accepted moves still lose with costs included |
| B4 | Share B3 engineering; test whether TV adds information beyond cooldown | Same useful decisions can be selected without TV, or stable cases are delayed without payback |
| Original dispatcher | Retain as correctness/control reference; avoid maintaining a second optimization branch | No execution headroom even with precomputed assignments |
| Parallel dispatcher | Locate the first-token delay, then test cheap static bypass; consider placement only after headroom | Oracle headroom is too small, or bypass/placement causes tail or workload regressions |

**A bounded next experiment**

Before changing policy, instrument a small pilot subset with correlated request IDs and monotonic timestamps at HTTP receipt, tokenization completion, DP selection/enqueue, engine admission, first execution and first streamed token. Use local durations and explicit clock alignment across processes/nodes. Add NVTX/CUDA-event ranges for routing, mapping, count maintenance, expert kernels, communication, proposal handling and commits. Nsight Systems or torch.profiler can expose dependencies; sampled Nsight Compute can examine achieved memory traffic and padded GEMM work. Profile only the diagnostic subset and compare against unprofiled execution so observation overhead is visible. A large NCCL duration may mean another rank is late, not a slow link.

The existing fallback-config audit also limits a tempting explanation: E16 versus E20 uses identical small-batch BF16 defaults. Only GROUP_SIZE_M changes for gathered M=2064–2579. A missing tuned-config warning does not prove a kernel defect. Collect the actual gathered/padded M distribution before any shared kernel tuning, and apply tuned configurations to OFF and the other arms fairly.

Keep the eight GPUs, pinned model, two datasets, one group, matched arrivals, fixed per-request lengths and existing negative results. Run small diagnostic comparisons first, select at most one candidate per necessary research question using pilot evidence, and freeze it before full measurement. Do not expand into a full Cartesian product. The existing final content has already been examined: another run on it is a targeted regression test, not a fresh generalization result.

For a claimed useful improvement, require at least 5% lower mean full response versus the relevant unchanged control and OFF, no more than 2% mean/p95 regression on either workload, exact output counts, all admitted requests completed and explicit reports of TTFT, native execution, queueing, elapsed/drain and throughput. These are decision margins; one group still supplies no uncertainty estimate or reproducibility proof. Preserve logical top-k choices and combining weights for matched inputs, token conservation, numerical tolerances, rank agreement and committed placement/weight consistency. An event after a copy is weaker than a full content check; asynchronous draining is not commit proof. Track unfinished control work separately so apparent savings cannot come from abandoning migration debt.

**Evidence scope and novelty**

The review draws on eleven conference papers with the project's existing methods/evaluation extractions, plus six relevant manuscripts examined here; it is a focused update, not a claim to have newly reread fifty papers. The evidence file records sections, configurations, artifacts and transfer limits. METRO, TEMPO and FreeBalance remain preprint evidence; Gimbal's publisher status remains unconfirmed despite its conference header. ELDR and MegaFlux are forward-citation additions, treated as preprints. Full methods/evaluation records for established papers remain in the linked prior matrices; their older references to a one-layer backend are superseded by this full-model analysis.

The citation search used primary reference lists and OpenAlex neighborhoods of METRO, MegaScale-Infer and PagedAttention. Metadata was screened rather than trusted automatically: unrelated Crossref title matches and an inconsistent reference title were discarded. Available project artifacts were inspected only to the extent recorded; no external paper's results were reproduced. [Evidence inventory](full-serving-optimization-evidence.json), [prior MoE extraction](generalization/literature-moe.json), [prior serving extraction](generalization/literature-serving.json).

The defensible research contribution is a measured operating boundary: when physical replica selection or migration actually repays its overhead in a pinned serving system. Improvement thresholds, fewer active copies, fused dispatch and migration budgets each have prior art. A TV gate becomes an additional contribution only if it predicts better realized decisions than cheaper controls. For now, the strongest result is that faster planning and fewer transferred bytes have not produced a practically meaningful, regression-free speedup over EPLB OFF.
