# Arrival timing diagnosis

The inconsistent response gains persist when the recorded coordinator gaps are removed in an accounting replay. The dominant opportunity is the long charged-service work at periodic native boundaries, with additional unexplained nonboundary service spikes. This diagnoses a service-path problem; it does not establish that GPU expert dispatch alone causes the reversals. All 128 archived trials satisfy the exact integer-nanosecond FCFS recurrence below. No original record, policy or execution source was changed.

For immutable arrival aᵢ, admission eᵢ, command start sᵢ and completion fᵢ, define readyᵢ = max(aᵢ, fᵢ₋₁), dᵢ = fᵢ−sᵢ, gᵃᵢ = eᵢ−readyᵢ and gᵈᵢ = sᵢ−eᵢ. Then fᵢ = readyᵢ+gᵃᵢ+gᵈᵢ+dᵢ, and response decomposes exactly into carried queueing readyᵢ−aᵢ, residual admission gᵃᵢ, dispatch gᵈᵢ, and service dᵢ. Stored admission delay eᵢ−aᵢ includes inherited FCFS backlog; it is not an estimate of OS wake-up lateness.

Across the native trials, residual admission averages 4.091 µs/job and dispatch 12.150 µs/job; online averages 4.118 and 11.988 µs. Removing every residual gap while freezing each recorded service vector lowers mean response by 0.114 ms for native and 0.102 ms for online on average. Initial-start lateness is even less influential: removing the first start gap changes mean response by only 1.199 µs and 1.137 µs, respectively. Initial admission lateness averages about 19.5 µs; maximum first start gap is 139.503 µs. Small gaps can propagate at high load, so their impact is larger than their direct duration, but they do not explain the key reversal.

For B=1,024 constant high-load arrivals, removing gaps leaves native/online response at 2.675/3.515 ms. For bursts it leaves 6.042/5.120 ms. The inconsistent direction therefore survives zero coordinator gaps. After gap removal, capping boundary excess reduces mean response by 1.770/2.416 ms for native/online constant arrivals and 2.057/1.267 ms for bursts. Removing gaps first versus capping boundaries first gives different marginal effects because queueing is nonlinear. The JSON retains both orders and their interaction; these contributions must not be presented as independent causal percentages.

The four boundary jobs account for 15.71% of native and 16.37% of online service time on average, despite being only 1.56% of jobs. Their duration above the within-run ordinary median totals 34.98 ms/native trial and 35.58 ms/online trial on average. Queue propagation amplifies that excess. The raw means at jobs 63/127/191 are 9.807/10.025/10.255 ms; terminal 255 averages 8.292 ms, versus 0.797 ms across other jobs. The source audit verified that native rearrange still performs observation aggregation/allreduce/CPU copies, zero-send movement planning, inverse-map reconstruction for 160 slots, map copies and commit synchronization when the candidate equals the current map. Terminal suppression skips the local default planner but not the native unchanged-map aggregation/commit path. These executed operations explain why an unchanged-map fast path is a concrete target, while the timestamps alone cannot apportion their individual costs.

The troublesome opt-pilot-01/domain 1, B=1,024, constantρ=.85 case is not simply a slight mean-kernel regression. Online response is 6.503/6.279 ms in the two repetitions versus native 2.450/3.770 ms. Online boundary service totals 51.25/51.44 ms versus native 33.49/43.65 ms. It also contains an ordinary job 133 service spike of 13.063 ms in repetition 0 and job 168 of 13.397 ms in repetition 1, plus 3.424 ms at job 194. The largest pre-start gaps in those trials are only 75.0 µs and 48.9 µs. Those unexplained delays are inside charged service, where host scheduling, Python/control work, GPU work, synchronization and communication coexist. Without a separately scoped profile they cannot be assigned to GC, clocks, the GPU selector or OS scheduling. Reverse-order repeats do not by themselves prove random noise or cancel these effects.

The proposed fix should preserve native observations, candidate planning, rank agreement, effective map/weight state and numerical outputs, while skipping movement and inverse-map commit only for a verified unchanged map. A small development contrast can isolate frozen native SKIP from the same dispatcher with this path; a separate dispatcher arm is unnecessary if the fix is generic and all routing stays native. Correctness must check exact physical/logical windows, unchanged maps, zero payload, same decisions and sampled numerics. Boundary/tail profiling should be separate from timed trials.

Fresh arrival validation should freeze the implementation, baseline-only capacity rule, calendar generator, all seeds, arm order, primary endpoint, guards and bootstrap before results are inspected. A bounded 256-trial design uses eight new calendar blocks assigned evenly to four reused source/domain states, two batch sizes, two load fractions, two arrival shapes, two arms and two reversed technical repeats. Its primary estimand can be the equal-cell high-load mean-response ratio, with low-load mean and high-load p 95 as guards. Calendar blocks, not jobs or repeats, are the uncertainty units. Stratified resampling within the four fixed payload states preserves their declared mixture; only two seed blocks per stratum still gives limited tail precision. The resulting intervals concern the declared arrival generator conditional on these payloads, calibration and allocation. They do not represent new documents, production serving traffic, or a hardware population.
