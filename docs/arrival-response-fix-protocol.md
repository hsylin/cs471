# Arrival-response no-op optimization protocol

This study will test whether avoiding redundant movement and commit work for an unchanged expert map reduces response latency under newly generated arrival calendars. The treatment will preserve native planning, physical replica assignment, observations, and fixed expert residency. It will be compared with the native SKIP path on the same two H100 GPUs, checkpoint layer, replica budget, payload sequence, and arrival calendar. The new randomized calendars will be held out from development. The captured model inputs will be reused and will not be described as fresh payloads, natural request traces, or previously unseen documents.

## System and intervention

The system will retain the existing pinned Qwen3-30B-A3B layer-0 BF16 weights, hidden size 2,048, expert intermediate size 768, 128 logical experts, top-eight routing, and 32 additional expert replicas. Two ranks will hold 80 physical expert slots each. The execution backend will remain replicated input with output all-reduce. The native vLLM source pin will remain `db9527a46873454610df6dbedf79a36d6bf1a7f6`. Batch size will mean the number of captured token inputs in one layer-service job, with values 128 and 1,024. Jobs will not be combined through inter-request batching. All planning and observation parameters will remain those of the frozen native SKIP configuration, including a 16-job observation window and a 64-job planning interval.

The treatment, named `fast`, will wrap the existing movement and commit operations. When the effective old and new maps are identical on all ranks, it will avoid redundant no-op movement work and inverse-map reconstruction while preserving the externally visible native state and return contract. Native planning, demand aggregation, forward mapping, observation recording, and ring progression will still execute. Rank agreement needed for this decision will be timed. A changed map or mixed rank decision will use the original operation on every rank. Unsupported asynchronous, profiling, resizing, or rank-remapping configurations will be rejected before measurement; an unexpected unsupported call will fail closed rather than enter an unreviewed fallback. The intervention will not replace native assignment with the earlier online resident dispatcher and will not bypass planning solely because the policy is SKIP.

Execution will require the two equivalent H100 94 GB GPUs allocated on `kh014`, with NV6 connectivity: `GPU-204281f9-9c87-3c25-adfb-9edd6c972ec3` and `GPU-7d5c9009-305b-9341-957f-1deb96611ed5`. Their identities and topology were checked from allocation 4872248 before measurement. The user authorized an equivalent pair and requested a 48-hour allocation; it runs from 9 November 2026 at 16:45:31 to 11 November at 16:45:31 Zurich time. The pilot, native calibrations and final comparisons will use this same held pair as ordinary sequential Slurm job steps. Rank-to-device assignments will be archived. A different pair will be rejected before measurement. The original exact-`kh083` request, job4872158, was cancelled while pending and produced no measurements. This hardware-only amendment was frozen before GPU results; the intervention, gamma weights, endpoints and statistical rules are unchanged.

## Timing and arrival definitions

A job's planned arrival time will be denoted by a. The coordinator's later head-of-line admission observation will be denoted by e. Service start s will be recorded immediately before the rank-0 CPU command, and completion f after both ranks acknowledge completion of their GPU work. All four timestamps will use the verified same-host monotonic clock contract. Response latency will be f−a, waiting time s−a, and service residence f−s. The admission observation e will not replace a when calculating latency.

Each trial will contain N=256 jobs. Native calibration capacity C will be calculated as N divided by the interval from the first service start through the final completion of a saturated native-only run. This denominator includes inter-job coordinator bookkeeping. The offered-load fraction will be denoted by rho and fixed at 0.5 or 0.85. The nominal exposure horizon H will be N/(rho C). The same native calibration for a payload state and batch size will determine both treatments' calendars. No treatment outcome will enter the calibration or calendar rule.

Each new calendar will use 256 positive gamma weights drawn from a frozen random seed. The gamma shape will be 4 for the spread pattern and 0.25 for the clustered pattern; scale will cancel during normalization. The first arrival offset will be zero. For job indices 1 through 255, the arrival offset will be H multiplied by the sum of all preceding weights, divided by the sum of all 256 weights. The final weight will therefore describe the unused gap between the final arrival and H. The binary64 gamma draws will be generated by Python `Random.gammavariate` and archived as hexadecimal strings before GPU execution. Each archived value will be represented as its exact binary64 rational number when calculating ideal offsets. The horizon and offsets will be rounded to the nearest nanosecond with ties to even. A rounded release equal to the rounded horizon will be capped at H−1 ns, retaining every job; this cap changes the rounded deadline by at most one nanosecond. Total deviation from an ideal rational offset can be slightly larger when horizon rounding is also involved. The resulting vector will be checked for ordered offsets within the horizon and archived before final treatment timing. Calendar seeds will be new unique deterministic derivations of the base identifier 20261109, with the derivation and resulting seed list recorded in the frozen manifest. The same dimensionless weight realization for a calendar block and pattern will be scaled across the two loads and batch-size calibrations. All paired arms and repetitions will receive identical resulting offsets.

These are fixed-count normalized gamma calendars. They will not be called unconditioned Poisson arrivals or natural production traces. Irregular gaps remove the fixed eight-job burst boundary that previously aligned with the 64-job planning cadence. No phase will be selected using observed execution times. The payload sequence itself will retain the same 32-input repetition used by the earlier validation harness; new calendar timing will not be presented as new or nonperiodic model demand.

The pinned vLLM serving benchmark similarly precomputes gamma gaps and normalizes fixed-rate cumulative deadlines. Its shape parameter controls arrival irregularity, and its wait is relative to cumulative deadlines. The present experiment adds an explicit immutable planned-arrival timestamp to its layer-service contract; the code's request-loop admission and separately tracked client queue time are not substituted for that timestamp. [Pinned vLLM benchmark](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/db9527a46873454610df6dbedf79a36d6bf1a7f6/vllm/benchmarks/serve.py)

An occupied server will not postpone the planned arrival sequence or omit jobs. All 256 jobs will complete in first-come, first-served order with one job in flight. Completion will include the CPU command, rank coordination, GPU synchronization, planner work, rank agreement, and terminal work. The complete horizon, drain beyond the horizon, wake-up lag, and observation overshoot will be recorded. The response convention follows the coordinated-omission principle of retaining latency from the time work was supposed to arrive. [wrk2 primary implementation](https://github.com/giltene/wrk2)

## Development and final matrix

Development will use the reused `opt-pilot-01/domain1` payload state at both batch sizes, the previous constant and burst-eight calendars at load 0.85, and three arms: native, an identical native implementation under the `native_alias` label, and fast. Two reversed-order technical repetitions will yield 24 trials. The alias arm will install no adapter and will follow exactly the native implementation. The alias/native timing difference will be reported descriptively, including every repetition and the reversed execution orders. The three-arm order keeps the native-alias label in the middle; this descriptive A/A comparison does not eliminate every possible position effect. The final two-arm comparison fully reverses both positions. It will not be an eligibility gate or certify a one-percent noise floor. Two repetitions over four cases do not establish that precision for response latency; completed elapsed time is also dominated by the imposed horizon and cannot substitute for it. This decision replaces the initially proposed numerical A/A gate before protocol freezing and before development measurements. Exact route, state, hash, and numerical correctness checks will remain mandatory. A structural or correctness failure will halt final execution until a documented repair and new source pin are reviewed. The final study will proceed regardless of the pilot's performance direction once these mandatory checks pass. The fast path is the single prechosen intervention; pilot outcomes will not select among online-dispatch, static-planner, or other alternatives.

A separate diagnostic will execute the observe-only native path and fast at both batch sizes for 128 jobs per trial. The observe-only path will perform the same equality and rank-agreement checks but still call the original movement and commit functions. It will measure CPU residence inside selected native stages to locate redundant work; its total time will not represent the unwrapped primary native baseline. These four diagnostic trials will be excluded from the response-latency comparison and will not be pooled with either development or final evidence. Stage residence can contain synchronization and launch effects and will not be interpreted as an exclusive GPU time share. No profiler timing will replace the uninstrumented final measurements.

The final payload states will be the four combinations of `opt-pilot-01` and `opt-pilot-02` with `domain0` and `domain1`. These are previously observed captured inputs. There will be eight new calendar blocks, assigned in advance as two distinct seed blocks per payload state. Each block will be crossed with the two batch sizes, two loads, two patterns, native and fast, and two reversed-order repetitions. The resulting matrix will contain 256 trials. Repetitions will reverse the order of the two arms while preserving their calendar and payload sequence; group order will be randomized by the frozen rule.

One final allocation on the required GPUs will first run eight saturated native-only calibration trials, one for each payload-state/batch-size combination. The deterministic calendar freezer will then write the resolved final manifest, including the calibration-record hashes and exact offsets, before any final treatment timing. All 256 evaluation trials will run in that allocation. This sequencing will preserve a fixed calibration rule while avoiding reuse of a capacity measured under a different allocation. All attempts and complete or incomplete records will remain archived. A missing or censored trial will prevent a complete-study acceptance claim; it will not be removed selectively from the denominator.

## Endpoints and uncertainty

For each trial, the arithmetic mean response latency and the empirical 95th percentile response latency will be calculated from all 256 planned-arrival-to-completion values. Empirical quantiles will use linear interpolation. The treatment ratio will be fast divided by native. Technical repetitions will be combined by averaging their paired log ratios; jobs and repetitions will not count as independent calendar blocks.

The primary endpoint will be the high-load mean-response ratio at rho=0.85. Within each calendar block, the four batch-size/pattern log ratios will receive equal weight. The eight block summaries will then receive equal weight, equivalent to equal weighting of the four fixed payload states. The exponentiated average will be the reported geometric mean ratio. The useful-gain target will require the upper bound of its two-sided 95% bootstrap interval to be strictly below 0.95.

Two aggregate guards will be assessed separately: the mean-response ratio at rho=0.5 and the response-p95 ratio at rho=0.85. Each guard will use the same equal weighting over batch sizes, patterns, and calendar blocks. Its upper one-sided 97.5% bootstrap bound will have to be at most 1.02. The two guard bounds apply a Bonferroni correction for their two-endpoint family. The p95 ratio will compare trial-level quantiles; it will not be calculated by pooling jobs across unrelated calendars. Study acceptance will require the primary target and both guards. A bound crossing a threshold will mean that criterion was not established. An interval lying wholly beyond the harmful side will be distinguished from an inconclusive guard.

Bootstrap resampling will draw two whole calendar blocks with replacement within each of the four fixed payload states. This will preserve the target mixture and all within-block pairing across loads, patterns, batch sizes, and repetitions. The analysis will use 100,000 resamples and seed 471420. Only two calendar blocks exist within each payload stratum, giving 81 distinct possible block-count combinations. A large resample count will not be described as additional independent information. The intervals will be conditional on the four reused payload states, native calibration, one allocation, and specified random-calendar generator. They will not support population claims about source documents, real serving traffic, or other hardware.

All eight batch-size/load/pattern cells will be reported descriptively. No cell-specific success or significance claim will be inferred from the aggregate acceptance rule. The report will also include service and waiting distributions, completed elapsed time, nominal horizon, drain, inter-job gaps, observed spikes, and actual time spans of observation windows. Host-server occupied time will not be called GPU utilization. Because exposure duration is controlled by offered load, a response-latency gain will not be relabeled as an equal throughput gain.

## Correctness and scope

The paired arms will retain identical checkpoint, resident maps, physical token assignments, combining weights, native planner inputs, decisions, logical observation windows, and final state. Raw assignment artifacts and observation windows will allow an independent verifier to check these invariants. The unchanged-map study must have zero movement bytes in both arms. Explicit changed-map and mixed-rank tests will verify that the wrapper takes the original native path when required. Numerical checks will use the existing pinned BF16 reference procedure and tolerances, without substituting synthetic routes or hidden states.

The existing warm-up, conditioning, and device-input materialization will be identical across arms and reported as excluded setup. All timing-critical operations of the evaluated service path will remain inside measurement. Source hashes, hardware UUIDs, calendars, raw timestamps, calibration provenance, and independent analyses will be archived. The completed historical studies and their frozen parameters will remain unchanged. A positive result will support an implementation improvement for unchanged-map work in this fixed layer-service setting; it will not establish a new balancing algorithm or a full-serving latency guarantee. A negative result will close this bounded intervention without changing its endpoints or adding further optimization families.
