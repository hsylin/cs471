SkewBench merits proceeding as a six-week course project, provided its claim is narrowed to the incremental value of a two-window stability gate and its observation/commit semantics are made explicit. Scientific motivation is sound; novel decision value is unestablished; implementation feasibility is conditional on an early distributed-layer prototype. No measured speedup, successful integration, or independent replication is established by this review.

The proposal is strongest where it separates balance from time. It includes an improvement-only baseline, fixed-budget comparisons, completed elapsed time including overhead, pilot/final separation, paired runs, identical logical routing, an always-skip control, correctness checks, and room for a negative result. Its example arithmetic is consistent. These are substantive controls, not omissions to be rediscovered. The remaining question is whether low adjacent-window total variation identifies better migration decisions than a simpler throttle.

The assessment concerns Sections II–VI of the supplied four-page proposal, SkewBench (3).pdf. Statements below distinguish retrieved source facts, deductions from the rule, and measurements still required.

**What the primary literature establishes**

Load balance is a means to reduce completed execution time, and the appropriate proxy depends on the execution regime. METRO changes physical replica dispatch while retaining EPLB placement and replication; it reports that token balancing can activate too many expert weights in memory-bound decode. Its hardware and simulator results should be distinguished, including its proprietary simulation component. TEMPO models activation cost, token work, and tile padding, and separates modeled improvements from hardware validation. These manuscripts justify calibrating the proposal's peak-token improvement against actual old-map/new-map times; they do not validate historical TV as a migration-payback signal. ([Yu et al. 2025, METRO](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.09277); [Li et al. 2026, TEMPO](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.13057))

Migration restraint is already part of related systems, but their interventions differ. Gimbal combines source-aware expert placement with frontend scheduling; its migration-stability term penalizes movement rather than comparing two demand distributions. FreeBalance predicts an upcoming layer's load from the residual stream, preserves final logical routing, and constrains swaps using transfer/overlap budgets per rank and link. Its fixed-swap-count comparison is a useful precedent for testing simple restraint. Neither establishes the benefit of SkewBench's exact rule with a native planner held fixed. The narrow novelty is a controlled admission-policy ablation, not migration-aware balancing in general. ([Sun et al. 2026, Gimbal](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.15177); [Chen et al. 2026, FreeBalance](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.14205))

METRO, TEMPO, and FreeBalance are treated here as preprints. Gimbal's manuscript names ATC 2026, but the retrieved record did not independently establish proceedings publication; it is cited as an arXiv manuscript. The reviewed versions were METRO v1, Gimbal v1, TEMPO v2, and FreeBalance v1. No exact two-window TV gate was located in these four papers; this bounded comparison cannot establish exhaustive novelty. No runnable public artifact was validated: no implementation or trace link was found in the three non-TEMPO fulltexts, while TEMPO's [linked repository](https://github.com/jeshxxx/TEMPO) returned 404. Artifact inaccessibility limits independent checking; it does not itself refute a manuscript's claims.

**Five ranked weaknesses**

1. **The mechanism is not identified by the current primary comparison. Necessary fix.** B4 versus B3 identifies the total policy effect of adding the gate under matched input and initial state. Its downstream maps and candidates may legitimately diverge. That is not a confound, but neither does it prove that TV carries useful decision information. A slowdown in migration frequency could explain a gain. Add one frozen cooldown baseline using the same improvement rule and candidate cadence. Diagnose a few identical-state allow/skip branches. If the cooldown performs equivalently, the distinct value of TV remains unsupported; wide uncertainty means inconclusive, not equivalence.

2. **Two past windows do not establish the future payback interval. Necessary stress tests.** Multiplying both windows' counts by a constant leaves normalized demand, TV, and predicted relative peak gain unchanged, although weight-copy costs need not scale with token work. Equal window histograms also discard within-window order. A sudden persistent change initially raises TV precisely when a new placement might become useful; a switch immediately after approval defeats the inference from past stability. These are mathematical limitations, not proof that the heuristic fails on relevant traces. Test transient bursts, persistent changes, and switching phase relative to the window. Report rejection delay and subsequent realized payback.

3. **The observation and rejection protocol is underspecified. Necessary feasibility gate.** The existing counters do not automatically supply two complete consecutive logical windows. Correct history must survive map changes, be global across ranks, and continue updating when migrations are rejected. Async rejection must complete its cycle without stranded events or inconsistent collective participation. Demonstrate forced allow, forced skip, alternating decisions, incomplete windows, and a delayed rank before collecting final timings. This is the most serious implementation risk, because a fast but semantically incomplete rejection path would invalidate the experiment.

4. **TV noise and the load proxy can masquerade as the desired mechanism. Necessary calibration.** With fewer assignments, two samples from the same stationary distribution can look less stable. Consequently, a fixed threshold can indirectly act as a batch-size filter. Separately, the predicted peak reduction assumes equal replica sharing and ignores actual kernel/communication behavior. On pilot traces, measure stationary false rejection, actual replica traffic, activated experts, and old-map/new-map time. Use these measurements to interpret the rule; do not add a new learned cost model.

5. **The success criterion is not yet operational. Necessary pre-registration.** Repeat variation alone is insufficient: steps within a run are correlated, absence of a significant slowdown does not establish acceptable regression, and fewer bytes are not saved seconds. Declare the independent sampling unit, final run count, primary aggregate, useful-gain margin, stable-workload tolerance, numerical tolerance, and completion boundary before final runs. Keep layer claims separate from serving claims. Full-model integration and broader hardware/model sweeps remain optional.

An illustrative deduction quantifies the sampling issue without pretending to measure this system. Under independent categorical assignments, equal window size N, and a uniform distribution over E experts, a normal approximation gives E[TV] approximately sqrt((E−1)/(pi N)). For E = 128 it is about 0.199 at N = 1,024 and 0.0248 at N = 65,536. These are analytical illustrations, not routing-trace observations. Top-k constraints, temporal dependence, and skew violate the approximation's assumptions; estimate actual false rejection from stationary pilot traces. The proposal's example threshold 0.05 is illustrative, not a claimed final setting.

**The smallest decisive experiment**

Fix one GPU allocation/topology, one real model-derived MoE layer, dtype and kernel, one execution mode, one native planner, a fixed replica budget, and both communication backends. Use the actual layer dimensions and weights, not a reduced shape presented as that model. Select two token-batch sizes from pilot measurements, one where movement/control costs are conspicuous and one with enough work to amortize them. Do not assume those sizes establish memory- and compute-bound regimes without profiling.

Retain B0 and B1 for a small contextual calibration of H1. Run the core matrix with B2, B3, B4, the proposal's always-skip control at the same replica budget, and one added B5 cooldown. B5 uses the B3 improvement test at the same proposal opportunities, but admits a proposal only after K engine steps since its previous accepted proposal; choose K from a small pilot grid and freeze it. Log actual commits and overlap. Do not retrospectively tune K to B4's final migration count. All methods share initial placement and eligible hardware resources; B0's no-replica setting remains explicitly contextual.

| Trace family | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Stationary skew with sampling noise | False rejection and stable-workload regression |
| A brief hotspot burst followed by reversion | Avoiding migrations whose benefit vanishes quickly |
| An abrupt change to a long-lived hotspot | The opportunity cost of waiting for two stable windows |
| Periodic switching, with two fixed phases relative to the window | Aliasing and dependence on window alignment |

Freeze the phase choices, hotspot durations, thresholds, cadence, workload horizon, and trace-generator seeds/split before final runs. Treat the phase variant as a small stress check rather than expanding every factor. Pilot and held-out traces must differ in actual seeds/realizations. Keep an even-load sanity check small. A recorded trace from the chosen model is useful if already feasible, but a new corpus suite is unnecessary; synthetic routing supports controlled layer-level claims, not evidence of production hotspot prevalence.

Choose a warm evaluation contract: identical kernel warmup, then two complete observation windows under the common starting placement before the measured horizon. State that cold-start behavior is excluded. Account for B4's ongoing collection and computation costs during timing. Stop admitting new proposals at the fixed input horizon, finish all accepted transfers through normal commit, synchronize relevant device completion, and include that tail. Do not insert per-step barriers that destroy native overlap. Use CPU wall-clock elapsed time for the completed horizon and report the slowest rank; device events and profiler timelines are explanatory diagnostics.

For the mechanism test, select approximately eight B3-improvement-eligible decision states from a predetermined common B3 reference trajectory, stratified by workload/batch and sampled before observing branch outcomes. Do not cherry-pick low-TV successes. Snapshot the same weights, mappings, two logical windows, candidate, random state, and future replay cursor. Restore each state twice: force allow in one branch and force skip in the other. Disable subsequent rebalancing in both diagnostic branches and replay the same fixed future inputs. Normal full-policy B3/B4/B5 runs retain subsequent decisions.

Define the realized local decision value as Y(H) = T_skip(H) − T_allow(H), including actual transfer/commit cost over the same H future steps. Positive Y favors migration. Plot these values against TV, marking the frozen threshold and showing predicted gain, batch size, and moved bytes so obvious cost differences remain visible. A few states diagnose failure mechanisms; they do not justify a universal predictive model. When stationary per-step timings are appropriate, C_exposed / (T_old − T_new) is a useful descriptive break-even estimate, only for a positive denominator. It is not a substitute for the branch measurements when costs overlap or demand changes.

The experiment most likely to change the verdict is this combined cooldown comparison and decision replay. If B4 wins only against B3, its added complexity may be generic restraint. If it also beats the cooldown and the replay shows that its rejected moves lose time while accepted moves repay their costs, the information-value claim becomes substantially stronger. Conversely, consistent rejection of profitable persistent-change moves would directly undermine H2's proposed explanation.

**Raw records, correctness, and acceptance**

The minimal event record needs a run/seed and engine-step identifier, layer and source rank, logical top-k IDs and combining weights, token order, actual physical-replica counts, window boundaries and valid counts, current/proposed/committed map identifiers, TV and predicted improvement, decision and reason, transfer bytes, enqueue/start/end/commit events, and completed timing. Keep replay tensors or a deterministic recipe/checksum. Save the source revision, model revision, dimensions, dtype, GPU/interconnect configuration, kernel, effective flags, and communicator versions. Diagnostic profiling should use separate runs; controller work that the real policy needs remains charged in primary timings. On CUDA, Nsight Systems/NVTX and PyTorch profiling can relate host control, transfers, collectives, and kernels; use backend-appropriate equivalents for ROCm. ([Nsight Systems guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-systems/UserGuide/); [PyTorch asynchronous timing](https://github.com/pytorch/docs/blob/site/stable/notes/cuda.md))

Use randomized, paired method order over independently generated trace seeds. Repeat identical traces when needed to quantify runtime noise, but cluster those repeats by seed. Pick final replication from pilot variance and the available run budget, then freeze it. Bootstrap whole seed blocks, preserving paired methods and workload cells, or use a justified paired run-level interval. Never bootstrap individual steps as if they were independent. Predeclare the short-lived-hotspot family as the primary useful-gain endpoint, with equal weight for the two batch sizes and both sizes also reported separately. Persistent changes and phase-aligned switching test operating limits; stable skew tests regression. Do not choose the headline regime after seeing results.

For a concrete pre-registration, I recommend a 5% minimum useful runtime reduction against both B3 and B5, and at most a 2% stable-workload regression relative to B3. These are reviewer-selected design margins, not literature constants or predicted outcomes. Require the upper 95% confidence bound on the paired runtime ratio T_B4/T_reference to be below 0.95 for the primary gain claim, and the upper bound to be at most 1.02 for the declared stable-workload noninferiority claim. Freeze these criteria before held-out measurements. If the feasible run budget cannot resolve them, report an inconclusive result rather than changing the margin after seeing results. A positive but smaller effect may still be publishable as a course finding if described accurately.

Correctness is a prerequisite: unchanged logical top-k IDs and combining weights; agreement with an independent fixed-placement layer reference under dtype-appropriate absolute/relative tolerances fixed on pilot data; exact preservation of expert identity and copied weight contents; consistent map inverses and replica counts across ranks; unchanged weights/maps after rejection; and successful subsequent cycles after rejection. Include all-allow, all-skip, alternating decisions, empty/incomplete windows, threshold boundaries, and a delayed-rank case. Existing [distributed EPLB tests](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/db9527a46873454610df6dbedf79a36d6bf1a7f6/tests/distributed/test_eplb_fused_moe_layer.py#L186-L256) offer a starting harness, but do not prove the new gate correct.

**Feasibility and final judgment**

The week-one gate should be concrete: demonstrate memory fit on the allocated GPUs, a native distributed-layer run, one correctly committed rearrangement, one coordinated rejection followed by another successful cycle, and correct logical-window counts. If async integration cannot meet that gate, explicitly choosing a synchronous path for every comparison can preserve a useful bounded study, with claims restricted to that path. It must not be presented as a result about default asynchronous behavior. Missing hardware or failure of the native layer harness is a genuine feasibility blocker; unspecified thresholds are routine pilot work.

Within six weeks, weeks 1–2 should establish this contract and freeze the pilot choices; weeks 3–4 should complete the gate, cooldown, branch replay, and correctness checks; weeks 5–6 should run the fixed experiment and reproduce its principal figure from raw logs. Learned prediction, dynamic replica budgets, multiple models, and end-to-end serving are optional extensions.

The project is scientifically sound as a test of a modest, falsifiable heuristic. Its incremental novelty is plausible but bounded, and its performance mechanism remains open. A strong result is either reproducible evidence that historical stability adds value beyond a cooldown, or a precise explanation of why it does not. The current proposal is suitable for that course-project goal once the five necessary fixes above are made.
