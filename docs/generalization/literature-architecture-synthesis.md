The next experiment should test whether the measured benefit survives new routing demand and new arrival structure while keeping the implementation fixed. The architecture literature supports reducing unnecessary work, but it does not support predicting latency from active-expert counts or padded FLOPs alone. The benchmarking literature also distinguishes fresh independent workloads from more repetitions of the same workload. Together, these findings justify a bounded generalization study with captured model inputs, a fixed comparator, fully charged online costs, and uncertainty computed across independent content/arrival blocks.

Reducing arithmetic work is useful only when the resulting execution still uses the hardware efficiently. FlashAttention preserves exact attention while exchanging recomputation for less HBM traffic; its BERT evaluation measures time to a shared accuracy target rather than treating an operation count as the outcome. FlashAttention-2 then improves the same calculation by changing sequence and warp partitioning, showing that an IO-efficient algorithm can still leave parallelism and shared-memory costs unresolved. FlashAttention-3 adds Hopper-specific asynchronous execution and separate mechanism ablations. Its appendix fixes clocks, averages 100 repetitions, and adjusts long sequence lengths to multiples of 132 to avoid wave quantization. These are relevant experimental controls, but those adjusted shapes should not silently replace naturally captured shapes in our workload. None of the three attention studies proves that our expert GEMMs are memory-bound. ([Dao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-1189), [Dao 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/98ed250b203d1ac6b24bbcf263e3d4a7-Abstract-Conference.html), [Shah et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-2193))

Sparse execution gives a particularly strong counterexample to treating less padding as a runtime bound. SparseTIR's relational-graph experiments explicitly add padding and still obtain faster kernels through better load balance and tensorization; its sparse-convolution comparisons also contain losses. Sputnik likewise reports configurations where vectorization is worse for small, occupancy-sensitive problems and a comparison where its SDDMM kernel reaches only 92% of a competing implementation's throughput despite using less memory. The appropriate inference for resident dispatch is conditional: concentrating a logical expert's tokens on an existing copy may reduce activated copies and padded work, but can increase a rank's useful token load or change occupancy and locality. We should retain the critical rank's actual duration, both ranks' token/active-copy/tile counts, and the same native kernel configuration. These counters explain an observed effect; they cannot establish a Tensor Core, HBM or SM bottleneck without the corresponding measurements. ([Ye et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3582016.3582047), [Gale et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/SC41405.2020.00021))

The cost of discovering and materializing a sparse execution plan belongs in the online treatment. PIT's evaluation separates static kernel tests, which omit conversion and compilation, from complete model runs that include dynamic index construction. Some baseline kernel savings disappear after conversion; PIT's own micro-tile search and indexing still have measurable costs. SparTA is complementary evidence about propagating sparsity and specializing execution plans, but it mainly handles compile-time weight sparsity and some experiments change accuracy through pruning or quantization. Its headline averages also exclude failed tuning cases. Neither is a justification for changing our checkpoint, top-k choices, or combining weights. They support retaining a clean distinction between an offline headroom experiment and the implemented current-batch selector, with histogram, selection, mapping and counter maintenance charged. ([Zheng et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613139), [Zheng et al. 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/zheng-ningxin))

Kernel tuning should remain a separate, finite development step. TVM demonstrates why operator fusion, layout conversion and graph-level effects need end-to-end validation; TensorIR shows that tensorization and schedule search can approach vendor libraries while still losing on some operators. Ansor makes the tuning objective and budget explicit and allocates effort to important subgraphs. Its operator error bars summarize variation across shapes, whereas one search comparison takes the median of five repeated searches: those are different sources of uncertainty. For our study, the resolved tile configuration, selection inputs and development budget should be pinned before evaluation. A configuration selected on new held-out outcomes would convert that evaluation into another tuning set. ([Chen et al. 2018](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi18/presentation/chen), [Feng et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3575693.3576933), [Zheng et al. 2020](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/zheng))

A faster layer is not yet a faster service. DeepSpeed Inference distinguishes latency-oriented generation, throughput-oriented generation and resource-constrained offload, using different prompt/output lengths and configurations. Its equal-tensor-parallelism dense comparison helps isolate kernel effects, but its generation latency is not an open-loop queue-response experiment. MLPerf makes that difference explicit: its server scenario uses Poisson arrivals and a throughput target subject to a tail-latency constraint, while single-stream and offline scenarios measure other behaviors. Its paper-era protocol requires a minimum duration and substantial query counts for tail guarantees. Our finite 256-request response and p95 measurements remain meaningful paired trace endpoints, but should not be presented as steady-state production tails or MLPerf-equivalent precision. Complete scheduled-arrival, start and finish clocks are necessary to distinguish service improvement from queueing and dispatch gaps. ([Aminabadi et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/SC41404.2022.00051), [Reddi et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCA45697.2020.00045))

Real conversations broaden content coverage without automatically providing representative serving traffic. WildChat collects opt-in interactions through a free API-backed service and reports substantial variation in language, topic, length and user activity. Its linking and anonymization methods also show why conversation IDs, user proxies and filtered text require explicit handling. Sampling disjoint conversation-derived prompts is a stronger test of new routing demand than resampling an old token bank. However, concatenating, truncating or teacher-forcing those prompts through one layer creates a constructed workload; WildChat timestamps cannot automatically be attached to that workload as an authentic production trace. A current filtered dataset release must have its own revision, filters and license recorded; the paper-era license does not establish the terms of a later release. Coding and mathematical prompts can add deliberate stress strata, but their inclusion does not by itself make the mixture representative of users. ([Zhao et al. 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/9421261e06f1a63a352b068f1ac90609-Abstract-Conference.html))

More repeated jobs cannot repair a narrow or biased experimental design. Mytkowicz and colleagues show that setup choices can reverse optimization conclusions even when each setup is measured precisely; their remedy combines setup variation with causal tests rather than merely collecting more iterations. Kalibera and Jones distinguish builds, executions and iterations, and allocate repetition to the level that contributes uncertainty. Applied here, this means retaining source-document/content-block identity, arrival-block identity and allocation identity, averaging technical repetitions within their parent block, and reporting uncertainty at the independently varied level. Eight arrival seeds over four reused content states support a conditional arrival claim; they do not establish eight independently sampled content populations. The methodological papers concern CPU/compiler and VM workloads, so this is a design lesson rather than a claim that their specific mechanisms explain our GPU noise. ([Mytkowicz et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1508244.1508275), [Kalibera and Jones 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2464157.2464160))

The strongest next claim is therefore deliberately local: the frozen exact-routing implementation improves a prespecified response endpoint on fresh, independently blocked content and arrivals for this model-derived layer, hardware, replica budget and execution path. Preserve all three existing arms if the question is incremental composition, use the same input and arrival tape within each comparison, charge every online selector and agreement cost, and publish per-domain and per-load outcomes alongside the prespecified aggregate. Keep mechanism counters and correctness checks separate from inferential endpoints. If a domain fails, retain that result and characterize the demand regime; changing the selector or choosing easier traces would require a new development round. A second model, real autoregressive decoding, different replica geometry, multi-node transport and production request mixing remain distinct claims for later studies.
