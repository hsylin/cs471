# Closest-work methods appendix

The relevant novelty boundary is exact physical execution under a fixed residency map. Existing work already minimizes activated experts, models nonlinear expert costs, restrains reconfiguration and removes scheduling overhead. The present follow-up tests the unchanged native/shortcut/combined implementation on new content and externally derived arrival calendars through the same pinned Qwen3-30B-A3B layer 0, B128, BF16, two H100s and 32 extra resident slots. It does not add another model, layer, kernel or transport. This appendix inspects ten close works: six conference papers already in the 50-paper inventory and four supplementary preprints. It adds no papers to that conference count.

## Dispatch objectives

### METRO

[METRO v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09277v1), an arXiv preprint, is the closest objective-level overlap. Section IV-A fixes expert placement and replication; Section IV-B reduces minimum maximum activated-expert count to a restricted matching problem. Algorithm 1 in IV-C assigns all tokens for an active logical expert to one eligible GPU, choosing by activated-expert load. Section V describes a single-SM CUDA implementation with shared-memory counters and ordered lock acquisition, incorporated into decode CUDA graphs. This is direct prior art for one-copy physical dispatch, rather than evidence that the present policy introduces that principle.

Its information and transport contracts matter. IV-C all-gathers tokens before top-k, duplicates the top-k computation and retains a combine communication phase. The present replicated-input harness already supplies globally identical logical routes; it does not measure the cost of obtaining that view from partitioned requests. Sections VI-A–C separate real eight-A100 vLLM results from proprietary B200 simulations. The useful adaptations are the restricted-replica feasibility check, exact token conservation and separate accounting of dispatch computation and communication. The present study can evaluate its own two-rank assignment and latency; it cannot claim to reproduce METRO, dominate its complete serving path, or obtain its reported speedup. No runnable METRO artifact was verified, so these are manuscript implementation points, not inspected source code.

### TEMPO

[TEMPO v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13057v2), also a preprint, is closest to the question of when an activation objective stops predicting runtime. Sections 3–4 combine an activation-dependent memory floor with token work and tile-rounded computation, then calibrate the deployed pipeline rather than only the expert GEMM. Appendix B lists fitted parameter sets; Appendix C logs bytes, fan-in/out and measured all-to-all time. Appendix D lists seven integration points, including expert-location initialization, recorder compatibility, a rebalance hook and recorder-buffer limits. These names are auditable manuscript details; the linked repository returned 404 in the earlier artifact check and the appendix still describes an anonymized artifact.

Sections 5.8–5.12 provide particularly useful controls. The small-expert BF16 configuration does not beat static placement; changing a refresher period initially changes Python GIL overhead, not demonstrated demand staleness. A full-path no-op, an out-of-process token-LP with the same machinery, and fresh versus deliberately stale placement separate objective quality from adaptation cost. The study also reports shared-machine drift larger than some effects. The current follow-up should retain matched no-op/component controls, all charged execution costs and unfavorable content cases. It cannot borrow fitted coefficients, treat 10/50/200-ms refresher results as evidence for an engine-step cooldown, infer GPU identities from anonymous testbeds, or label a lower activation count as a calibrated runtime optimum.

### MegaScale-Infer

[MegaScale-Infer, SIGCOMM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3718958.3750506) provides conference precedent for a nonzero cold-expert cost. Section 6 of the [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02263) defines each node's cost as a sum of expert allocation fractions multiplied by the larger of token-computation cost and a cold-expert floor. The allocation fraction is not a binary activated-replica indicator. Thus this model anticipates nonlinear expert cost, but it is not identical to METRO's activation objective or TEMPO's per-replica fixed-charge model. The accompanying greedy plan uses traffic from a prior period and permits redundant deployment.

Its larger intervention includes attention/FFN disaggregation, ping-pong microbatches and CPU-proxy many-to-many RDMA. Section 6 describes fused gate/top-k/count/scatter preparation and Flux-based collective/GEMM fusion. Section 7/Table 4 uses BF16 larger-expert models and a private production trace; serving throughput is optimized under a token-latency target. There is no inspected complete public system/trace artifact. The reusable evaluation idea is to state the bottleneck model and distinguish single-batch latency from throughput under a constraint. The current fixed-layer experiment cannot test disaggregation or claim the cold-expert floor as a new insight; its measured allreduce path remains distinct from this system's communication design.

## Adaptation and controls

### Gimbal

[Gimbal v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15177v1) is retained as an arXiv manuscript: its internal venue label is not sufficient evidence of proceedings publication. Sections 4 and 5 coordinate DP request scheduling, queue ordering and expert placement. The source-DP-to-expert matrix retains traffic origin, unlike a single aggregate popularity vector. Section 5.2 explicitly penalizes movement from the current placement; 5.3 scores feasible placements using communication, load and migration terms, prefers no movement in ties, and migrates only when the selected placement differs. Section 6 calibrates score weights against an offline MINLP reference, while 7.2 compares calibrated and uncalibrated heuristics.

The overlap is restraint against unproductive movement and the need to include front-end effects, not an exact adjacent-window total-variation rule. The source-aware matrix cannot be meaningfully reconstructed from an all-inputs-replicated two-rank job without a genuine partitioned source model. The reusable control is an explicit changed-map versus unchanged-map path, with the planner retained and commit cost charged, plus clearly separated calibration and evaluation inputs. Neither its calibrated numerical coefficients nor whole-system TTFT/TPOT improvements should be imported. No primary implementation or raw request artifact was verified, and a held-out calibration split should not be assumed where it is unreported.

### FreeBalance

[FreeBalance v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14205v1), a preprint, predicts the target layer's current-batch demand before that layer's router executes. Algorithm 1 and Section 3.3 then consider pairwise expert swaps. The objective combines maximum rank load and load dispersion; nonpositive predicted improvements are removed. Each swap's cost includes a fixed launch/protocol term and transferred bytes divided by measured link bandwidth. Available attention time minus a safety margin bounds transfers per rank and per link. Deterministic benefit/cost ordering and lexicographic ties make every rank reproduce the same conflict-free plan; swaps preserve slot count and avoid extra replication.

This is already explicit cost-constrained migration admission. Its residual prediction is not a prediction that yesterday's popularity will persist, and its reported 95% successful cases concern complete executed layer plans, not individual swaps. Section 4.3's fixed-swap ablation is useful evidence that moving more weights need not improve runtime. The current implementation can reuse determinism, rank agreement, fixed-capacity checks and the rule that a predicted improvement is insufficient without measured cost. It cannot claim attention-overlapped migration: weights remain resident and preceding attention is outside the timed layer job. No runnable public artifact was verified; the budget and tie details come from the manuscript.

### SmartMoE

[SmartMoE, ATC 2023](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc23-zhai.pdf), gives direct threshold/cadence ancestry. Section 6.2 discusses gain thresholds, proximity to the current mapping and empirically selected online-search frequency; 7.4 examines search overhead against stale placement. The [AE repository](https://github.com/thu-pacman/SmartMoE-AE/tree/e19b3a994db5f7614778fd02c48f602ba9d9ac6e) exposes a narrower concrete behavior in `src/fastmoe/fmoe/smartmoe/mapping.py`: `greedy_gen_mapping_from_history` processes historical expert counts in descending order, prefers the previous owner on an equal-load tie, and returns a new mapping only when the previous maximum exceeds the new maximum multiplied by 1.0. That inspected function has no adjacent-window TV test or exposed positive margin. The broader paper description should not be substituted for this exact implementation.

`plotting/from_logs/fig12.py` selects one static and one dynamic archived log and plots per-layer ratios. That enables arithmetic inspection, not an independent workload uncertainty estimate. The reusable ideas are a fixed planner, explicit no-change branch, source-preserved demand and paired complete execution. Its training parallelism search and mutable placement are outside the current resident inference study. A no-op shortcut preserving the original planner can be a defensible engineering change, but movement-aware admission and avoiding unnecessary remapping are not novel principles. Current code and archived logs were read; no AE run was performed.

### Tutel

[Tutel, MLSys 2023](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5616d34cf8ff73942cfd5aa922842556-Abstract-mlsys2023.html), separates adaptation from parameter conversion through a common layout. Its Appendices A and B specify hierarchical all-to-all and sparse fast encode/decode; Appendix C.2 changes expert count, top-k and capacity while reporting quality as well as speed. In the [inspected current source](https://github.com/microsoft/tutel/blob/f6610fd55b6b694ad9f033306b87cf37468f87c3/tutel/impls/moe_layer.py), `MOELayer.forward` computes router scores, calls `extract_critical` with explicit capacity/alignment, encodes, selects adaptive execution and performs all-to-all/compute overlap. It is not an external fixed-top-k replica dispatcher without adaptation.

Two implementation details constrain reuse. The current `SKIP_MOE` branch returns the input and bypasses expert computation; it is unrelated to this study's native SKIP placement policy and is not a valid correctness-equivalent baseline. `tests/test_tutel.py` compares example losses, with dtype-dependent rounding; that is weaker evidence than checking each captured batch against the exact pretrained expert computation. The useful adaptations are explicit layout/parallelism identity and measuring dispatch preprocessing alongside kernels. Current repository code differs from the 2023 artifact, so its API availability does not reproduce the original scale claims or establish compatibility with the fixed 160-slot layout.

### Lina

[Lina, ATC 2023](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc23-li-jiamin.pdf), combines communication scheduling with inference expert-path prediction, hot-expert replication and cold-expert packing. Sections 5–6 describe predicted path execution and weight prefetch; mismatches require corrective work before dependent computation. Section 7.1 is an especially useful timing specification: training averages 50 iterations after 10 warmups, while five inference executions are divided into two unprofiled latency measurements and three runs collecting profiler statistics. Sections 7.3/Figures 16–19 examine prediction and path-length components, and Table 6 changes tasks.

That protocol directly supports keeping Nsight diagnostics separate from primary response latency. Its ideal balanced-gate reference changes the workload and must not be presented as a realizable exact-routing baseline. The current study instead retains authentic logical IDs, physical-counter conservation and the same weights across all three arms. It can evaluate the cost of its own mapper and native no-op path under fixed residency, but not Lina's prediction accuracy, offloading benefits or full-model queue behavior. No dedicated public Lina implementation was verified; method and timing detail here comes from the proceedings full text, not a claimed code replication.

## Expert execution

### SonicMoE

[SonicMoE, ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Abstract-Conference.html), has both exact-execution and routing-changing components. Sections 3–4 reduce activation IO and schedule gather/GEMM/activation work; Section 5 and Appendix G discuss token rounding, which changes assignment. The extended manuscript's Appendix B compares kernel designs, C details gradients, and F–I contain further performance/quality settings. Training/backward and large-token results are not evidence of a B128 inference gain, and later Blackwell results must not be attributed to Hopper BF16.

At [commit 7396f3e](https://github.com/Dao-AILab/sonic-moe/tree/7396f3e604827d8186c2e16e64b28ee33d3defd0), the inspected `moe_general_routing_inputs` interface accepts explicit token indices, expert indices and routing scores. Its contract requires sorted token indices, declares interleaved gate/up weights by default, and supports an explicit concatenated layout. It builds routing metadata and invokes separate up/down projections. These are concrete compatibility points for a future backend adapter, not proof of a drop-in replacement. The authorized generalization study changes no backend. It should preserve the current BF16 path and cite SonicMoE as prior compute optimization, not claim a kernel innovation or apply token rounding to the frozen routes.

### MegaBlocks

[MegaBlocks, MLSys 2023](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5a54f79333768effe7e8927bcccffe40-Abstract-mlsys2023.html), replaces fixed expert-capacity padding with block-sparse execution. Sections 5 and 6 distinguish metadata construction, full training and isolated kernels. Section 6.3 averages 100 kernel executions under uniform routing but explicitly excludes sparse-metadata construction, arguing that it is amortized over six operations. The retrieved manuscript has no appendices. Its microbenchmark therefore cannot be treated as all-overhead inference latency. Section 6.2 also identifies small microbatches and 128-row padding as a remaining inefficiency; a smaller tile is a hypothesis, not an automatic speedup.

The [current `dmoe.py`](https://github.com/databricks/megablocks/blob/952db33d6eac334d22c61e47a0d5d41446298784/megablocks/layers/dmoe.py) sets `blocking=128`, rounds expert counts in `indices_and_padded_bins`, and separates `sparse_forward_once` from `grouped_forward_once`. The grouped path gathers, executes the MLP and scatters without the same block-sparse topology. `tests/layers/dmoe_test.py` includes BF16 comparisons with dense/other MoE implementations, but these do not cover the present distributed replica map. Reuse the separation of routing metadata, compute and reconstruction, while counting all of them in completed latency. Do not equate the modern grouped path with the original block-sparse paper or infer a memory-bound condition solely from a padding proxy.

Across these works, the most transferable improvement is experimental attribution: preserve the operation, charge its complete implementation, expose unchanged and unfavorable cases, and report the domain actually tested. The new content/arrival experiment can test portability across prespecified inputs at one fixed layer and hardware pair. These papers motivate the checks; they do not supply external replication of the measured result or extend its claim to full autoregressive serving.
