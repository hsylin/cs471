# Strengthening the combined-path result

The completed held-out experiment finds **7.8% lower mean layer-job response latency beyond the unchanged-map shortcut**, with a conditional 95% interval of **6.8–8.8% lower**. The useful improvement to the research was stronger workload evidence and attribution while keeping the dispatcher fixed. The earlier combined-path result was 10.6% lower under fresh synthetic calendars over reused payloads; it supported that fixed-workload comparison, not unseen-content or production-serving generality. The new result retains the same checkpoint, placement and two-H100 allocation while changing content and arrival evidence. MBPP's point gain is only 3.7%, and completed elapsed improves about 0.06%, so the aggregate result does not imply a universal 5% benefit or a comparable throughput increase. [Previous measured result](https://github.com/hsylin/cs471/blob/71a1baaccc7ce3e4ccc81429c354ce35624132b3/docs/arrival-combined-results.md).

The review covers 50 conference papers: 18 MoE systems papers, 17 serving papers, and 15 architecture, workload and measurement papers. The searchable matrix and JSON preserve methods, configurations, data construction, baselines, timing boundaries, units, uncertainty, ablations and limitations. Conference identity and accessible manuscript version are separate: some retrieved texts are author manuscripts, and byte-identical camera-ready equivalence is not assumed. The ten-work appendix concentrates on the closest mechanisms and artifacts; relevant preprints and access-limited conference candidates remain supplementary rather than filling the count.

A contribution based on better physical execution is credible, but recognizing expert activation cost is not new. MegaScale-Infer's cost model already places a floor on cold-expert cost; its allocation-fraction formula is not identical to a binary charge per activated replica. METRO's preprint is closer to activation-aware physical dispatch, but changes global-information transport. TEMPO's preprint combines memory- and compute-sensitive cost terms. Our bounded contribution is a fully charged implementation and an incremental comparison against an already optimized shortcut, with explicit operating limits. It should not claim to invent activation-aware scheduling. [MegaScale-Infer](https://doi.org/10.1145/3718958.3750506), [METRO preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09277v1), [TEMPO preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13057v2).

Prior work also makes migration cost part of the decision. SmartMoE uses predicted improvement, current-placement preferences and a cadence trade-off; its inspected implementation must be distinguished from the paper's broader discussion. Tutel changes compatible execution modes without always relocating parameters. Gimbal and FreeBalance are relevant preprints about stability and transfer constraints, but their mechanisms do not establish that a two-window TV gate predicts realized payback. The earlier negative TV-gate and migration-payback results therefore remain evidence, not obstacles to remove from the narrative. [SmartMoE](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc23/presentation/zhai), [Tutel](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5616d34cf8ff73942cfd5aa922842556-Abstract-mlsys2023.html), [Gimbal preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15177v1), [FreeBalance preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14205v1).

The strongest disagreement in the architecture evidence is with a simple “less padding means faster” explanation. SparseTIR sometimes adds padding and improves execution through balance and tensorization. PIT separates static kernel timings from complete execution with dynamic indexing. MegaBlocks' sparse-kernel microbenchmark excludes sparse-metadata construction; its complete-training result has a different timing boundary. SonicMoE distinguishes exact IO/layout changes from token-assignment rounding that changes routing. These findings favor measured service and critical-rank work over an arithmetic proxy, and disallow adopting route-changing optimizations under an exact-router claim. [SparseTIR](https://doi.org/10.1145/3582016.3582047), [PIT](https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613139), [MegaBlocks](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5a54f79333768effe7e8927bcccffe40-Abstract-mlsys2023.html), [SonicMoE](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Abstract-Conference.html).

The serving evidence gives the same warning at a larger boundary. Orca, PagedAttention and Sarathi-Serve use synthetic arrival processes in relevant evaluations, even where request lengths come from real workloads. InferLine preserves matched traces and separates profiling from evaluation, but its reconstructed arrival details remain transformations. Production-derived lengths, synthetic timestamps and recorded timestamps answer different questions. Azure's code/conversation data provide the third category, but contain no customer prompt text, so their original content–arrival correlation cannot be recovered by pairing them with WildChat or benchmark questions. [Orca](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yu), [PagedAttention](https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613165), [Sarathi-Serve](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/agrawal), [InferLine](https://doi.org/10.1145/3419111.3421285), [DynamoLLM](https://doi.org/10.1109/HPCA61900.2025.00102).

Public artifacts require their own execution audit. The inspected BurstGPT example revision converts timestamps to consecutive gaps and then starts all tasks together, each sleeping its gap; that example does not preserve an absolute calendar. This is not evidence that the published KDD experiments used the defective revision. The correct transfer is to use raw external timestamps with our audited absolute-clock replayer. Likewise, NVIDIA's Wide-EP and vLLM's large-scale-serving articles discuss all-to-all traffic, overlap and larger deployment geometries; DeepEP is a communication implementation, not evidence that our replicated-input/output-all-reduce path has measured those benefits. [BurstGPT](https://doi.org/10.1145/3711896.3737413), [pinned example loader](https://github.com/HPMLL/BurstGPT/blob/d895a53bb7b8ec137d0d2fe203b335835a78c10a/example/src/burstgpt/workload.py#L82-L93), [pinned example runner](https://github.com/HPMLL/BurstGPT/blob/d895a53bb7b8ec137d0d2fe203b335835a78c10a/example/src/burstgpt/runner.py#L48-L60), [NVIDIA Wide-EP](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/scaling-large-moe-models-with-wide-expert-parallelism-on-nvl72-rack-scale-systems/), [vLLM Wide-EP](https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-12-17-large-scale-serving), [pinned DeepEP](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepEP/blob/f6aa38fe06cd4a0489e7730365d4e0114970a2f3/README.md).

The measurement literature supports more independent workload evidence rather than more repetitions of a small bank. Mytkowicz and colleagues show that precise measurements can still be biased by setup; Kalibera and Jones allocate repetition to the level that causes variation. MLPerf separates offline throughput from open-loop response and uses substantial query counts for statistically supported tail-latency checks under its specified query model. Our 256-job p95 remains a finite-trace paired endpoint. Neither hundreds of jobs nor 100,000 bootstrap draws create more independent content groups, production dates or hardware allocations. [Mytkowicz et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/1508244.1508275), [Kalibera and Jones](https://doi.org/10.1145/2464157.2464160), [MLPerf Inference](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCA45697.2020.00045).

The three research changes, in priority order, are:

1. **Test new content against external calendars.** Pin the filtered WildChat release, MBPP descriptions and GSM8K questions; exclude answers and generated continuations. Deduplicate both full documents and the model-visible truncated prefixes before splitting. Keep one content-independent placement. Use separately pinned contiguous Azure blocks, preserving ties and exact scaling. B128 is the main shape so the budget supports more content and date coverage while testing the known small-batch risk.
2. **Explain charged gains and losses.** Retain native, shortcut and combined arms; record per-expert sizes, activated copies, tile padding, rank maxima, mapper cost, periodic stalls, service and queueing. Use separate diagnostics for precomputed assignments, routing-only length matching and input/control alignment. An offline selector cannot become a deployable baseline, and an operation count cannot stand in for a measured critical path.
3. **Add a fallback only if pilot evidence justifies it.** A reproducible negative regime may justify one cheap pilot-selected rule, with its collection/decision cost charged and the unchanged combined arm retained. Otherwise keep the algorithm and improve the operating-limit explanation. The completed pilot did not identify such a failure, so the final study freezes the existing dispatcher without a fallback.

Implementation has already exposed two validity issues that the original prompt should make explicit. Full-text deduplication missed three near-duplicate captured prefixes, including one pilot/final pair. The revised source selection passes an independent re-tokenization/prefix audit across all 1,920 selected documents. Separately, the Azure2024 code file covers May 10–16 and conversation May 12–18; the common eight-date sampling frame initially contemplated does not exist. The adopted split is temporal within each service, not a global future-date holdout. These are source-level findings, discovered before treatment timing.

The statistical claim is consequently conditional on the selected external trace material. Whole content bundles are resampled within dataset, retaining both arrival types, both rate definitions and all paired arms. Shared calendar dates are deleted together in a separate sensitivity analysis; that range is not an interval for unseen production days. The main test requires the combined/shortcut 95% upper ratio bound below 0.95. Seven prespecified 2% guards cover each dataset under each rate definition and aggregate calibrated-rate finite-trial p95; all use the declared multiplicity correction. Date-deletion robustness and correctness must also pass before the hierarchical comparison against native SKIP opens. The rates include both a common pilot-derived absolute rate and per-content native-calibrated 0.85 load.

Implementation and final timing are complete for the main comparison: 72 pilot trials and 216 held-out trials, with authentic capture from 1,152 final documents and 384 pilot documents. The frozen variance-only rule selected 18 final groups; all seven regression guards and seven shared-date deletion checks pass. Independent execution/statistical implementations and a clean archive extraction reproduce the main result exactly. These are same-team checks, not external-laboratory replication. A separate 24-trial diagnostic rerun corrects a discovered checksum defect; its first 24 trials remain ineligible and retained, keeping the total at 336 executions within the 384 budget. The corrected diagnostics pass strict route, timing and float-weight checks. They expose sequence sensitivity and 11–28 microseconds per job between the charged online path and its precomputed-assignment reference, but that difference includes adapter effects and is not an isolated selector-kernel measurement. No new algorithm is selected from these descriptive checks.

The causal evidence has a clear boundary. Under this shape, each activated copy consumes one M tile, so copy count and padding reduction are the same proxy. They do not measure utilization. Response, service and queueing differences are descriptive timing identities; content, dataset and assigned-arrival variation are not independently randomized factors. The routing-only first-eight-token-per-document comparison retains substantial cross-dataset routing differences, but is not a matched-latency test. The diagnostic controls support a limited mechanism account without producing a validated online workload classifier.

The main hypothesis would fail its practical-gain test if the incremental interval does not clear 5%; it would remain unsupported on a guarded workload if its 2% safeguard is inconclusive or fails. A statistically established regression, incorrect routing/weights, missing completion or inconsistent committed state would be stronger negative evidence. Lower imbalance, fewer activated copies or lower service time cannot rescue a failed response-latency result. Any positive conclusion remains about constructed fixed-token layer jobs on this allocation. It does not establish autoregressive request latency, all-to-all serving, later layers, a second checkpoint or new hardware.
