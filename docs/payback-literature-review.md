**SkewBench should become a study of when expert rebalancing pays for itself, with a small runtime-aware policy only if the measurements reveal useful actions.** I would stop treating the two-window TV gate as the central contribution. Keep the two H100s, one Qwen3-derived MoE layer, fixed replica budget, native execution, and paired replay machinery. Change the objective, the workload evidence, and potentially the candidate placements. This is a stronger six-week systems project than another threshold sweep.

The completed experiment motivates this recommendation without proving that all balancing is useless. On burst traces, B4/B3 elapsed time was 1.0040 with a 95% interval of [0.9990, 1.0098]; B4/B5 was 1.0116 [1.0094, 1.0136]. All eight forced migration branches lost time over the tested 128-step future. Those branches contained no profitable move, so they cannot establish that TV separates good moves from bad ones. A longer horizon, a different candidate, or real routed inputs could change the result. The old experiment remains a valid negative result for its stated configuration; it should not be relabeled after developing a new policy. [Completed evaluation](./final-evaluation.md).

**The literature changes the research question.** There are three different decisions: where expert weights reside, which resident copy executes a token, and when weights move. SkewBench originally held the first decision largely fixed and modified the third. That restriction was useful for isolating TV, but it also meant that a gate could only accept or reject the native planner's candidates. It could not repair a token-count objective that chose a slow placement, or choose a smaller, cheaper change.

Token balance is an imperfect latency proxy. METRO argues that spreading tokens across replicas can activate excessive expert weights during memory-bound decode; it changes replica dispatch and also changes communication, so its whole-system gains do not isolate the dispatch objective. TEMPO models expert activation, token work, and tile padding. These papers support measuring actual physical execution rather than dividing logical demand equally across copies. Neither establishes that a new latency model will improve our particular layer. The reviewed versions remain preprints: METRO v1 and TEMPO v2. [Yu et al., METRO](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.09277); [Li et al., TEMPO](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.13057).

Migration cost and forecasting are already crowded research areas. Gimbal includes a penalty for changing placement alongside placement and frontend scheduling. FreeBalance predicts upcoming demand from residual streams and limits swaps according to transfer and overlap budgets. PROBE similarly uses a lookahead predictor and coordinates replication, assignment, and prefetching. Their information and available overlap differ from two adjacent historical windows in a standalone layer. Borrow their insistence on charging movement and prediction costs; do not reproduce their entire prediction pipelines. Gimbal is cited here as an arXiv manuscript with an author-reported ATC 2026 designation; FreeBalance and PROBE are preprints in this review. [Sun et al., Gimbal](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.15177); [Chen et al., FreeBalance](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.14205); [Zhu et al., PROBE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00509).

The closest newer work also limits the novelty of a generic payback formula. Libra, published at ICLR 2026, predicts future expert demand and overlaps local computation with remote dispatch/sharding; its artifact documents the combined intervention. The full methods PDF was unavailable in this review, so the detailed mechanism assessment relies on those implementation documents. vExpert explicitly models compute and communication benefits against H2D transfer and contention costs, then allocates virtual expert slots under overlap constraints. Its November 4 arXiv manuscript reports NeurIPS 2026 acceptance; proceedings publication was not independently established here. Neither system is the proposed two-H100 fixed-budget experiment, but “consider costs before moving experts” is not a new mechanism. [Yang et al., Libra](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/9ff1ac9a659085fed0735362cafe5e53-Abstract-Conference.html); [Libra artifact](https://github.com/SNU-ARC/Libra); [Wang et al., vExpert](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06057).

Temporary replication offers another action, but also comes with established mechanisms. Least-Loaded Expert Parallelism, published at ICML 2026 with public benchmark code, spills hot-expert work to less-loaded ranks while enforcing capacity and minimum-GEMM-size constraints; it can fall back to ordinary EP. UltraEP, reviewed as a preprint, jointly chooses temporary replicas and token quotas while accounting for planning, movement, communication, and compute. This suggests measuring whether a smaller action beats a large native remap. It does not justify adding temporary slots beyond our fixed memory budget or importing a rack-scale transport. [Nguyen et al., LLEP](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/nguyen26m.html); [LLEP code](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/LeastLoadedEP); [Wei et al., UltraEP](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.04101).

CPU/GPU offloading is a legitimate but different project. Fiddler compares CPU execution with fetching weights, while ProMoE and MoE-Infinity exploit prediction and caching under memory pressure. Their relevance is the cost of a late or unnecessary prediction, not a ready replacement for resident expert balancing. Fiddler is an ICLR 2025 paper; the reviewed ProMoE and MoE-Infinity versions are preprints. I would leave this direction outside the current project unless constrained GPU memory becomes the explicit research target. [Kamahori et al., Fiddler](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.07033); [Song et al., ProMoE](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.22134); [Xue et al., MoE-Infinity](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.14361).

The defensible contribution is therefore narrower: determine which balance proxies rank realizable placements correctly, separate execution benefit from movement cost, and show whether a cheap policy can exploit that benefit on held-out routing traces. This is modest algorithmic novelty but a credible experimental contribution. A careful negative boundary is an acceptable course result; a new algorithm's superiority is still a hypothesis.

Their large system improvements combine several interventions, so the reported speedups are not forecasts for a placement gate. The transferable lesson is to measure physical expert activation, per-expert token sizes, and completed rank latency. Communication claims must match the harness. Meta describes all-to-all costs including host work and handle exchange; AMD distinguishes replicated-input TP+EP/all-reduce from request-sharded DP+EP/all-to-all. Our current harness executes identical inputs on both ranks and reduces their outputs. It can study placement, physical replica choice, and native weight migration on that path. It cannot establish improvements to a production all-to-all serving path. **The raw material should improve before the controller does.** Capture a small prompt/session set through the actual Qwen checkpoint, retaining layer inputs, logical top-k IDs, combining weights, token order, batch boundaries, and prefill/decode labels. Use authentic router outputs as the primary new workload; retain stable, transient, persistent-change, and window-aligned synthetic traces as controlled stress tests. If capture uses teacher forcing or reconstructed batches, label that precisely. A one-time capture does not require making full-model serving the performance target.

Public traces are useful for external comparison but are not automatically executable replay tapes. *Patterns behind Chaos*, published at ISCA 2026, provides routing-trace artifacts covering several large MoE models. The inspected Hugging Face dataset requires access acceptance; its Qwen trace is Qwen3-235B, not our Qwen3-30B, and the card describes expert IDs with decode capped at 128 tokens, not the hidden states and combining weights needed for our numerical replay. Use it, if accessible, to test statistical conclusions about demand; do not silently relabel it as native 30B traffic. The paper's GPU MoE-computation improvements must also remain distinct from its wafer-scale results and from end-to-end serving speedup. [Yu et al., Patterns behind Chaos](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCA66397.2026.00021); [reviewed v5 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05497v5); [trace dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/core12345/MoE_expert_selection_trace).

**The first experiment should separate available benefit from its price.** Select a few states by a fixed rule across the two batch sizes and workload regimes. For each state and the same candidate map, restore identical weights, maps, routing tape, and future inputs, then compare these three branches:

| Branch | Timed work | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| Keep | Execute the future with the current map. | What happens without this move? |
| Preinstalled candidate | Install and complete the candidate before timing; time its future execution. | Can this particular candidate reduce execution time if installation were free? |
| Charged candidate | Start from the current state; time native movement, normal commit, and future execution. | Does the candidate repay its actual installation cost? |

The free-install branch is a diagnostic, not a deployable baseline or a universal oracle. Use declared common warmup and cache-conditioning rules, balance branch order, repeat pairs, and include all later work. A migration can perturb caches, so the difference between free and charged branches need not equal transport duration alone. Test the existing native candidate first. A small prespecified alternative set may include a historical-vector placement or a legal single-copy change, but report every candidate tried. Use one short horizon and one longer pilot-frozen horizon to avoid interpreting the old 128-step loss as a lifetime result. Do not choose the reported horizon after seeing the final sign.

If preinstallation does not improve execution, admission cannot rescue that candidate; revisit the objective or report no measured headroom in the tested candidate set. If preinstallation helps but charged movement loses, the movement size and amortization horizon are the relevant obstacles. If charged moves reliably win in some states and lose in others, a selector has a measurable task. This three-branch test is the experiment most likely to change my recommendation.

**The preferred improvement is a small runtime-aware placement policy, conditional on that test.** First calibrate the existing fused-MoE kernel at the exact shapes, dtype, and routing sizes, including any supported tuning shared by every baseline. Record tokens per physical expert, active physical experts per rank, padded tile work, actual sender plans, local copies, transferred bytes, and planning/commit costs. Nsight Systems with NVTX can diagnose host synchronization, launch gaps, and overlap; Nsight Compute can check whether the active-weight proxy corresponds to the observed kernel bottleneck. Profile separately from primary latency runs so profiling overhead is not mistaken for policy cost.

Use one small past-only model in units of time. Its decision score can be written as estimated cumulative execution savings over a declared horizon, minus observation, planning, and exposed movement/commit costs. A positive margin admits the action. Validate the score's sign and ranking on held-out candidate pairs; a good fit to average kernel latency is insufficient. Preserve absolute demand and per-step structure, which normalized TV discards. Actual physical dispatch should replace the equal-sharing assumption in calibration. Uncertain future persistence remains a limitation: identical observed histories can have different futures, so no historical statistic can guarantee the correct decision at their divergence point.

If native candidates have poor payback, permit a very small candidate set: keep, the native proposal, and a few legal single-copy replacements. Such a replacement may overwrite a redundant cold expert only when another valid copy remains; all candidates preserve every logical expert and the fixed physical-slot budget. Freeze the shortlist rule and computation budget on pilot traces. This changes the action as well as the gate, so an overall policy win must not be attributed solely to admission. Include a candidate-only ablation on a small replay subset, or retain the native candidate throughout if isolation is the priority. Minimal movement itself is already implemented in related systems; the new evidence would be its measured usefulness under the chosen cost model.

The alternative is fixed-resident replica dispatch if the diagnostic shows useful dispatch headroom but unprofitable movement. That avoids predicting a migration lifetime, but LPLB, METRO, and TEMPO already cover much of the idea. In our replicated-input harness, every rank must make the same physical assignment for a token; independent random decisions can duplicate or omit work. Compare the native hash with one cheap deterministic cost-aware rule and charge its counting and decision costs. Do not build this alternative and the migration policy in the same six-week project. Cross-layer predictors, new asynchronous transports, shared-expert Waterfill, and a second serving stack should remain optional future work.

**A bounded schedule and an external verdict keep the pivot testable.** Week 1 establishes real routing capture and profiles the common baseline. Week 2 runs the three-branch decomposition and makes a declared continue/stop decision. Weeks 3–4 implement and validate only the selected intervention. Week 5 freezes it and evaluates fresh held-out prompt/session blocks; week 6 packages independent reproduction and writes the result, including negative cases. If the diagnostics show no robust benefit, use the remaining time to explain and reproduce that boundary rather than search indefinitely for a winning trace.

Keep B3, the existing simple cooldown B5, and always-skip at the same replica budget as primary controls. B4 can remain a historical reference. If workload changes justify retuning a cooldown, use only the new pilot and freeze it once; never tune it on final traces. For example, four primary policies, two batch sizes, ten held-out session blocks, and two repetitions produce 160 runs. That is an illustrative bounded design, not a power guarantee; pilot run-level variance determines whether the uncertainty is adequate. Repetitions and tokens do not replace independent workload blocks.

Predeclare a practically meaningful target, for example at least 5% lower completed elapsed time than both B3 and B5 on the targeted changing workloads, with no more than a 2% stable-workload regression. Require the paired run-block intervals to support the chosen margins, report always-skip explicitly, and preserve all planned strata. These are project decisions, not constants supplied by the literature. Charge online observation, planning, synchronization, transport, normal commit completion, and device completion. Lower token imbalance, fewer copies, or a positive predicted score is not success. If gains occur only against a weak baseline, or disappear against cooldown/skip, the claimed policy benefit fails.

Correctness evidence should include preserved logical routing and combining weights, token conservation, numerical comparison on real activations, replica-weight checks, inverse-map consistency, and rank agreement after normal commit. Draining asynchronous work is not evidence that its mapping and weights were committed. Publish immutable code/environment pins, the permitted replay data or a capture recipe, candidate and decision logs, raw branch/run timings, and an independent analysis command. An independent person should be able to recover the same conclusions; our own rerun alone does not establish external reproducibility.

The source selection includes one backward/forward citation step from ProMoE and Patterns behind Chaos, with capped index results; it is not an exhaustive priority search. Public code was inspected, but no newly reviewed system was installed or benchmarked. Missing artifacts limit reproduction: the earlier TEMPO link was inaccessible, and no runnable artifact was validated for the other three original comparison manuscripts. An accessible repository alone is not evidence that its paper's experiment can be reproduced. Detailed source pins and retrieval metadata are retained in the [evidence inventory](./payback-literature-evidence.json).

My ranking of the consequential changes is: **first**, prove that useful actions exist; **second**, use authentic routing and a calibrated execution objective; **third**, change the candidate set if accepting native proposals cannot help; **fourth**, keep the runtime and hardware scope compatible; **fifth**, make the novelty claim about causal evidence and decision quality. The first two are necessary. A smaller candidate policy is conditional. Replica dispatch, cross-layer forecasting, and transport replacement are alternatives or extensions. The project is scientifically sound and course-sized under this plan, but no literature source or completed result yet establishes that the proposed new policy will be faster.
