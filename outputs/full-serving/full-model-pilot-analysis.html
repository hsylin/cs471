Full-model resident pilot: no consistent advantage over OFF

All 240 timed-pilot executions completed (3 arms ×5 sources ×2 passes ×8 requests). These are previously used pilot inputs, one fixed-order server session per arm, at 2 requests/s. Pass1 repeats pass0 inputs without logprobs. No interval, p-value, final-data analysis or superiority claim is made.

On pass1, resident mean response is slightly lower than OFF on WildChat and InstructCoder, almost unchanged on NuminaMath, and higher on ShareGPT and LongBench. None shows a 5% mean-response gain over OFF. A lower mean can coexist with worse tails and longer completion time: InstructCoder illustrates this directly.

Mechanism evidence is limited: resident has a 1.60–2.42% lower TPOT estimate than placement-only on the first four sources, while LongBench is 1.29% higher. Against OFF, the first four differ by only −0.27% to +0.44%; LongBench is 5.99% higher. This is compatible with recovering a small cost of hash dispatch on the replicated map, without an established advantage over OFF. It does not isolate a kernel effect because scheduling, generated work and fixed execution order also differ.

Output length matters: ShareGPT resident produces 8.42% more tokens than OFF while mean response rises 8.12% and mean TPOT is almost unchanged. NuminaMath resident and OFF have similar total token counts, but OFF hits the 2,048-token cap once and resident never does; resident elapsed falls 15.03% while mean response rises 0.39%. InstructCoder resident mean falls 1.82%, but p95 rises 9.89% and elapsed rises 9.53%. The distribution and timing of individual outputs matter, not just their sum. These are descriptive associations, not causal decompositions.

Repeated inputs and cache

For pass1, prefix-cache hit/query counts match exactly across the three arms for every recorded engine/model label, and all recorded preemption deltas are zero. Total cached/query token counts are WildChat 11,792/11,860, ShareGPT 12,736/12,791, InstructCoder 1,104/1,177, NuminaMath 864/943, and LongBench 81,456/81,531. The second pass is strongly cache-warmed, especially for long prompts. Matching counters reduce one observed arm difference; they do not make this a cold-input test or prove identical cache execution. Pass0 and pass1 cannot be interpreted as a controlled cache experiment because diagnostics, repeated content, warming and generated outputs change together.

Both 160-slot arms use 11.32 GiB per GPU for model loading versus OFF's 9.63 GiB, an observed +1.69 GiB. Extra storage reduces capacity headroom, but the pilot records zero preemptions; these logs do not establish memory pressure as the latency cause.

What remains unresolved: equal-prompt runs generate different continuations; temperature zero does not guarantee identical text. These pilots cannot distinguish numerical/scheduling effects from routing overhead, cannot establish a causal benefit from the dispatcher alone, and have no independent session-level uncertainty. The frozen held-out experiment is needed for generalization. No policy, threshold or final selection was changed for this analysis.
