The literature paragraphs in `outputs/skewbench-technical-review.md` are faithful to the four retrieved primary manuscripts. No substantive correction is needed. Their publication and artifact qualifications should survive into the measured report. This audit evaluates source attribution; it does not replicate the papers or inspect the final GPU outcomes.

| Primary source and reviewed version | Exact overlap and boundary |
|---|---|
| Yanpeng Yu et al. (2025), **Efficient MoE Serving in the Memory-Bound Regime: Balance Activated Experts, Not Tokens**, [METRO v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09277v1) | Keeps EPLB placement and replication fixed and changes physical replica dispatch for decode. It supports the concern that token balance can increase activated experts and hurt time. Section IV-C also replaces all-to-all dispatch with all-gather; a headline gain is therefore not a pure objective ablation. Section VI combines real 8-A100 experiments with proprietary 8/16-B200 simulation. It neither tests historical TV nor admits weight migrations. |
| Yifan Sun et al. (2026), **Coordinated Scheduling for MoE LLM Serving**, [Gimbal v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15177v1) | Sections 5.2–5.3 explicitly penalize changes from the current placement, combine communication/load/migration scores, and break ties in favor of avoiding movement. Section 7.2 discusses overreaction to short-window load imbalance. This is direct prior art for migration restraint, but its “migration stability” is a movement penalty, not adjacent-window demand stability. Frontend scheduling and source-aware placement also change, so whole-system gains cannot isolate admission. |
| Jie Li et al. (2026), **TEMPO: Makespan-Aware Expert-Parallel Load Balancing Across Memory- and Compute-Bound Regimes**, [TEMPO v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13057v2) | Prices activation floors, token work, and tile padding for replica dispatch. Sections 5.8–5.12 distinguish policy quality from GIL, collective, and remapping costs; some configurations lose to static placement. The 10/50/200 ms refresh experiment attributes its effect to in-thread solver overhead, not evidence for a migration cooldown. These results support measuring total completed time and charging control costs. Hardware identities are anonymized; do not infer specific GPU models. |
| Pengfei Chen et al. (2026), **FreeBalance: Pre-Routing Online MoE Load Balancing via Residual Workload Prediction**, [FreeBalance v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14205v1) | Predicts the current batch’s upcoming layer load from residual representations. Section 3.3 discards nonpositive predicted gains and admits deterministic, slot-preserving swaps only within profiled attention-overlap budgets per rank and link. It avoids extra replication. Section 4.3 compares adaptive budgets against forced fixed swap counts, supporting a simple-restraint control but not implementing an engine-step cooldown. Its reported 95% success concerns complete layer swap plans, not individual swaps. |

METRO, TEMPO, and FreeBalance remain cited as preprints in this bounded review. Gimbal’s manuscript names ATC 2026, but the retrieved record did not independently establish proceedings publication; “arXiv manuscript” is the supported label. Reviewed versions are explicit above, rather than claims about later revisions. No implementation/trace link was found in the METRO, Gimbal, or FreeBalance fulltexts. TEMPO links [jeshxxx/TEMPO](https://github.com/jeshxxx/TEMPO), which returned 404 in the earlier check. These are artifact-access limitations, not findings that code cannot exist or manuscript claims are false. No runnable public artifact from these papers was validated.

Three points should constrain the final measured verdict:

1. **State the tested execution path precisely.** The experiment is synchronous native vLLM EPLB on pretrained Qwen3-30B-A3B layer 0, with controlled synthetic routes, Gaussian hidden states, replicated global inputs, and output allreduce. This can establish a policy effect for that layer harness. It does not establish full-serving TTFT/TPOT, normal token all-to-all performance, production hotspot prevalence, asynchronous EPLB behavior, or superiority over the four complete systems. Two token batches also do not by themselves establish memory- versus compute-bound regimes.

2. **Keep the contribution narrow and the cooldown comparison decisive.** Migration costs, movement penalties, and budgeted admission already exist in the primary literature. The bounded search located no exact two-window TV admission rule in these four papers; this is not an exhaustive novelty proof. The defensible contribution is the held-out, fixed-planner test of whether this particular historical signal improves completed runtime beyond improvement-only admission and a pilot-selected cooldown. Fewer migrations or a better token-imbalance score alone cannot establish that value.

3. **Separate performance acceptance from mechanism evidence.** Apply the frozen burst useful-gain and stable-regression criteria without choosing a favorable regime afterward. Failure to establish a 5% useful gain is not automatically evidence of slowdown; wide intervals are inconclusive. The eight fixed-horizon allow/skip states are descriptive and share a seed across strata, so they can expose a failure mode but cannot establish general predictive accuracy. The four papers do not validate low past TV as future migration payback, and none independently validates these measured results.
